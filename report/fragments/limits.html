Dataset generations & corrections

This is the study's second-generation dataset at two full replicates per configuration: every active record came through the shipped, reproducible runner (Claude Code headless path with answer validation and retry), and each config's tasks — 12 originally, 15 since the hard tier — were run twice (sample tags cc1/cc2; a few cells carry a third sample from the hard tier's n=3 pilot and haiku@medium's early self-test). Fable 5.1 (2026-09-01→02), Opus 5.5 (2026-09-26) and Sonnet 5.5 (2026-09-28→10-03, all 15 tasks) were added after the fact through the same runner at n=2, and T13–T15 were run across every config over 2026-09-27→10-03; one Fable 5.1 max-effort replicate hit a burst of transport failures that were quarantined as invalid and refilled on a later pass. The first-generation records (a session-bound orchestration harness) are preserved in archive/workflow-runs/. Replication materially revised three of our own published claims — Opus 4.8@low's perfect sweep, Sonnet@xhigh's ledger crossing, and "max effort never wins" — all documented in F4/F5. Two grading corrections carry forward: JSON-wrapped answers are graded on content, and transport failures are quarantined as invalid rather than scored. Two cost corrections landed 2026-09-27 and re-priced every record from stored token usage: Sonnet 5 was billed at $3/$15 instead of its $2/$10 list price (all Sonnet costs fell ~25%), and cache writes under the Claude Code harness were billed at the 5-minute rate (1.25× input) when claude -p writes 1-hour caches (2× input), which raised every other family's sweep cost 6–43% (most at low effort, where cache writes dominate). Neither correction moved any floor. The runner now also records which model actually served each attempt and quarantines records answered by a fallback model after a safety-classifier refusal — a failure mode discovered during the hard-tier tournament (see candidates/TOURNAMENT.md on the hard-tier-candidates branch).

Limits

n=2 is still small — ranges are honest but confidence intervals are not computable; the plan is to accumulate replicates over time (one runner/sweep.py --sample auto per pass). All runs came from one account on subscription serving, in four measurement windows: the original six families within three days of each other in July 2026, Fable 5.1 five weeks later, Opus 5.5 four weeks after that, and Sonnet 5.5 plus the whole hard tier over 2026-09-27 → 10-03 — so serving-side drift between windows is not controlled, and cross-window comparisons carry that caveat. Two harness changes landed with the last window and apply to every record made from 2026-09-27 (all of Sonnet 5.5, all of T13–T15): claude -p runs with an empty tool list, which removes ~20k tokens of Claude Code's own system prompt from each call — worth about $0.13 (Haiku), $0.36 (Sonnet), $0.90 (Opus 5), $1.05 (Opus 5.5), $1.25 (Fable 5) and $1.63 (Fable 5.1) per 10-task sweep at list price, so Sonnet 5.5's core-set costs are ~$0.36 lighter than a like-for-like Sonnet 5 row; and a harness budget overrun — the 128k output-token maximum or the runner's one-hour wall clock — is recorded once as a valid null answer (score 0) instead of being retried and quarantined, because a model that cannot finish inside the budget is a measured outcome. Six hard-tier cells carry that outcome: Sonnet 5 @ max on Quarry Duel (both replicates) and TALLY-12 (one), Sonnet 5.5 @ max on TALLY-12 (both) and Quarry Duel (one). The CLI did not yet know Sonnet 5.5's price at measurement time; its cost rows use the published $2/$10 list price. Costs are imputed from measured tokens at standard list prices; every family's price row and the 1-hour cache-write rate were verified against the CLI's list-basis cost fields, and recomputed costs match the CLI's own total_cost_usd to the cent. Tasks are synthetic, single-shot, ≤35 minutes, without repository context; long-horizon autonomy and agentic orchestration are not measured. Response-length headroom was raised to the models' own output ceilings; harnesses with tighter caps will fail the biggest grinds. Usage-window exhaustion during long sweeps produces transport failures — the sweep driver cleans and refills them automatically.
