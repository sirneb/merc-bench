Editorial synthesis of the graded data (charts and map regenerate automatically; this prose is curated). Every configuration now carries two full replicates through the reproducible runner; the retired first-generation dataset (a non-reproducible orchestration harness) is preserved in archive/workflow-runs/.

F1 · Most tasks saturate — then the cheapest config strictly wins

Knowledge recall, classic-bug review, state simulation, 5-attribute puzzles and regex writing were solved by essentially every configuration in every replicate; math, code trace, CSV and the constraint gauntlet sit within a one-or-two-point noise band for everything above Haiku. Same-score price spreads run 5–12×. For transforms, extraction, lookups, format work and classic review: buy the cheapest configuration that has passed the task for you.

F2 · Reliability floors, measured with replicates

Defining a config as clean only if it dropped zero points in every replicate of the 10 core tasks: Opus 5 is clean from medium up ($2.76, 9 min — the cheapest clean config until Sonnet 5.5 arrived); Opus 5.5 from xhigh up ($3.08, 10 min); Fable 5.1 from xhigh up ($8.03, 16 min); Sonnet is clean only at max ($3.55, 33 min); Fable 5 only at max ($11.53, 27 min); Opus 4.8 and Haiku were never clean at any effort. Sonnet 5.5 (added 2026-09-28) is clean from xhigh up ($1.29, 9 min) — now the cheapest replicated-clean config in the grid, with the caveat that it ran on the tools-disabled harness (≈$1.65 like-for-like; still cheapest). Four of the twelve replicated-clean configs in the entire grid belong to Opus 5; the three point releases added later (Fable 5.1, Opus 5.5, Sonnet 5.5) contribute two each.

F3 · Opus 5 was the value frontier of the core set; Sonnet 5.5 undercuts it and the hard tier dethrones it

On the ten core tasks Opus 5 is clean twice at medium, high, xhigh and max; at low it dropped exactly one gauntlet point — the same point — in both samples, while still solving the deep puzzles 6/6 and the subtle bugs 5/5 twice. Every other family of the July grid got less reliable under replication; Opus 5 got confirmed, and until September it was the single configuration to run for everything hard. Two things changed. Sonnet 5.5 (2026-09-28) is clean twice at xhigh for $1.29 — under half Opus 5@medium's price at the same 9 minutes — and is 5/5 on the subtle bugs at every effort where Sonnet 5 managed 4/5 below high; its own ladder is low: ledger 5/6 + gauntlet 9/10 twice, medium: ledger 4–5/6, high: one gauntlet and one puzzle cell in one replicate, then clean. And on the hard tier (§3b) Opus 5 collapses on cold-chain in one of every two runs at every effort while Opus 5.5 holds all three tasks from high. The measured answers are now: Sonnet 5.5 @ xhigh for core-shaped work at the lowest price, Opus 5 @ medium if you want the Opus tier's margin on the ledger wall, Opus 5.5 @ high when the work looks like long exact simulation. Opus 5.5, measured two months later at a lower list price ($4/$20), lands 32 cents behind it: clean at xhigh ($3.08, 10 min) and max, with one gauntlet point dropped in one replicate at medium and high and a ledger point (4–5/6) at low. Below its floor it is the fastest thing in the grid — a full low-effort sweep is 3 minutes and $1.78, a quarter less than Opus 5@low — so the choice between the two Opus generations is "clean at medium for $2.76" versus "nearly clean at low for $1.78".

F4 · Effort buys the last point — at the wrong price

Replication revised our own earlier claim that max never wins: max is precisely where Sonnet 5 and Fable 5 first went zero-defect in both samples (Sonnet 5's xhigh and Fable 5's xhigh each still dropped a point in one replicate). The hard tier then revised it back for the Sonnet tier: at max both Sonnets overrun the output or wall-clock budget and score zero on tasks they partly solve at medium (H3). But the last point is overpriced: Fable 5's ladder to its clean config runs $4.98→$11.53, Fable 5.1's $4.99→$8.03 (its slip clears one step earlier, at xhigh), Sonnet 5's $1.42→$3.55, Sonnet 5.5's $0.56→$1.29 for xhigh and $6.80 / 61 min for max (12× low, five times its already-clean xhigh, for identical core scores — and on the hard tier max is where Sonnet 5.5 stops answering TALLY-12 at all), and Opus 5.5's $1.78→$3.08 for xhigh and $7.71 for max. Opus 5 delivers replicated zero-defect at $2.76/9 min, Sonnet 5.5 at $1.29/9 min. Below the last point, effort mostly bought nothing; on Haiku it correlated with worse ledger scores (best sample 5/6 at low vs 2/6-band at high). Escalate one step when a config fails; buy the tier, not the grind, when you need clean.

F5 · Single-sample crowns die under replication

Three cc1 headliners regressed at n=2: Opus 4.8@low's perfect sweep (second ledger sample: 3/6 — its range is now 3–6 at every effort), Sonnet@xhigh's ledger crossing (5–6), and Haiku's crisp "effort inversions" (now wide variance bands: T3 2–5, T6 7–13, deep puzzles 3–6 at high). What replicated instead: Opus 5's cleanliness, the E generation wall, and Fable's stubborn single gauntlet point at every effort below max — a slip Fable 5.1 then reproduced at every effort below xhigh. Treat any n=1 surprise as a hypothesis; this repository exists so you can rerun it.

F6 · Cheap per token ≠ capable, but it is genuinely cheap

Haiku was never clean and its variance bands are the widest in the grid, yet a full 10-task sweep costs $0.93–1.21 — a fraction of the nearest floor — and inside the saturated zone it matched every tier in both samples. Route mechanical work there at low effort; never route precision aggregation or deep deduction there.

F7 · The bill is verbosity: effort multiplies cost for the same scores

Within every family, sweep cost rises 1.7–12× from low to max (Sonnet 5 $1.42→$3.55; Sonnet 5.5 $0.56→$6.80; Opus 5 $2.51→$4.30; Opus 5.5 $1.78→$7.71; Fable 5 $4.98→$11.53; Fable 5.1 $4.99→$12.00) and time rises with it (Sonnet 5.5: 3→61 min; Opus 5: 7→18 min; Opus 5.5: 3→33 min; Sonnet 5: 11→33 min) — almost always for identical scores. Sonnet 5.5 is the sharpest example: max costs 5× its already-clean xhigh and takes seven times as long for the same 100/100. Grind-prone precision work belongs at Sonnet 5.5's or Opus 5's floor — or give the model tools: in the archived tool-enabled rounds, execution access saturated these tasks for every tier.

F8 · The generation gap lives in novel semantics — and in reliability

Opus 4.8 stalls at 4/5 on the subtle semantic bugs at low and high and fixes the fifth only at xhigh — in all four samples across both study generations — while Opus 5 and Fable found 5/5 at every effort, twice. And at the same list price as Opus 5, Opus 4.8 never produced a clean sweep. The same-price generation upgrade is free reliability; take it and re-find your floor a step or two lower. The point releases replicated the pattern in miniature: Fable 5.1 kept 5/5 on the subtle bugs at every effort and moved Fable's clean point down one step (xhigh instead of max); Opus 5.5 also found 5/5 at every effort, but its cheaper list price is paid back in effort — it needs xhigh to be clean where Opus 5 needs medium. The Sonnet generation went the other way: Sonnet 5.5 finds 5/5 on the subtle bugs at every effort where Sonnet 5 stalled at 4/5 below high, and moves the Sonnet floor from max ($3.55) to xhigh ($1.29) at the same list price. Re-find the floor after every release, in both directions.

F9 · When the tasks stop saturating, the ranking changes

Everything above was measured on tasks where the frontier scores 98–100 and floors are decided by single points. The hard tier (§3b) asks the same question with headroom, and the answer is different: Opus 5.5 is the only model clean on all three hard tasks in both replicates (from high; $2.37 / 13 min for the three), Fable 5.1 gets there only at xhigh for 4–5× the price, Sonnet 5.5 holds cold-chain for $0.20 but never Quarry Duel, and Opus 5 — the core-set value frontier — collapses on cold-chain in one of every two runs at every effort. Sonnet 5 degrades above medium and at max simply fails to answer within the output budget. The core floors are still the right answer for work that looks like the core tasks; for long exact simulation, read H1–H5.
