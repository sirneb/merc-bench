Editorial synthesis of the graded data (charts and map regenerate automatically; this prose is curated). Every configuration now carries two full replicates through the reproducible runner; the retired first-generation dataset (a non-reproducible orchestration harness) is preserved in archive/workflow-runs/.

F1 · Most tasks saturate — then the cheapest config strictly wins

Knowledge recall, classic-bug review, state simulation, 5-attribute puzzles and regex writing were solved by essentially every configuration in every replicate; math, code trace, CSV and the constraint gauntlet sit within a one-or-two-point noise band for everything above Haiku 4.5 (Haiku 5.5 included: its only misses on the saturated ten outside the ledger are single points, once each). Same-score price spreads run 5–12×. For transforms, extraction, lookups, format work and classic review: buy the cheapest configuration that has passed the task for you.

F2 · Reliability floors, measured with replicates

Defining a config as clean only if it dropped zero points in every replicate of all 13 core tasks, exactly four configurations in the grid qualify: Opus 5.5 at xhigh ($7.22, 32 min) and max ($13.43, 65 min), and Fable 5.1 at xhigh ($19.61, 48 min) and max ($26.44, 67 min). Nobody else is ever clean. The barrier is almost always cold-chain (T14): Opus 5 holds it in one replicate out of two at every effort, Fable 5 never holds it twice, Sonnet 5.5 holds it at medium, xhigh and max but then drops TALLY-12 or Quarry Duel, and Opus 4.8 never exceeds 78 % on it except once. Haiku 5.5 is never clean for a different reason: it drops a ledger point at every effort, and from high its thinking exhausts the 128k output cap on TALLY-12 and Quarry Duel — yet at xhigh and max it holds cold-chain in both replicates, which only seven other configurations do, for $0.04 a run. On the ten original tasks alone the picture was far flatter — Sonnet 5.5 @ xhigh ($1.29), Opus 5 @ medium ($2.76), Opus 5.5 @ xhigh and Fable 5.1 @ xhigh were all "clean" — which is precisely why those three simulations were added: they turn single-point noise into a ranking.

F3 · Opus 5.5 is the value frontier; Opus 5 and Sonnet 5.5 are the value frontier of the saturated ten

Opus 5.5 is clean on all 13 tasks twice at xhigh and max, and at high it misses only one gauntlet point in one replicate while holding all three simulations ($4.77, 19 min) — the cheapest "nearly clean" config in the grid by a wide margin. Fable 5.1 reaches the same place at xhigh for 2.7× the money and 1.5× the time. Below them the ordering is by what each model cannot hold: Opus 5 is clean on the ten saturated tasks from medium and on TALLY-12 and Quarry Duel from xhigh, but cold-chain is a coin flip at every effort; Sonnet 5.5 is clean on the saturated ten from xhigh for $1.29 and on cold-chain from medium, but never holds Quarry Duel and stops answering TALLY-12 at max; Fable 5 clears everything but cold-chain at xhigh; Sonnet 5 peaks at medium and degrades; Haiku 5.5 at medium ($0.54, 53 min for the 13 tasks) scores 86–89 % on TALLY-12 and 48–100 % on cold-chain — Sonnet 5.5@low territory at a third of the price — before the output cap takes its simulations away from high upward. If you run one configuration for everything, Opus 5.5 @ xhigh is the measured answer; if your work looks like the saturated ten, Sonnet 5.5 @ xhigh at a sixth of the price. Opus 5.5, measured two months later at a lower list price ($4/$20), lands 32 cents behind it: clean at xhigh ($3.08, 10 min) and max, with one gauntlet point dropped in one replicate at medium and high and a ledger point (4–5/6) at low. Below its floor it is the fastest thing in the grid — a full low-effort sweep is 3 minutes and $1.78, a quarter less than Opus 5@low — so the choice between the two Opus generations is "clean at medium for $2.76" versus "nearly clean at low for $1.78".

F4 · Effort buys the last point — at the wrong price

Replication revised our own earlier claim that max never wins: on the ten saturated tasks, max is precisely where Sonnet 5 and Fable 5 first went zero-defect in both samples (Sonnet 5's xhigh and Fable 5's xhigh each still dropped a point in one replicate). The hard tier then revised it back for the Sonnet tier: at max both Sonnets overrun the output or wall-clock budget and score zero on tasks they partly solve at medium (H3). But the last point is overpriced: over the 13 tasks Opus 5.5's ladder runs $3.20 (low) → $7.22 (xhigh, clean) → $13.43 (max, still clean, nothing gained); Fable 5.1's $10.42 → $19.61 (xhigh, clean) → $26.44; Fable 5's $11.19 → $50.69 at max without ever going clean; Sonnet 5.5's $1.69 → $11.24 (≈ — three capped max cells could not be priced), with max the step where it stops answering TALLY-12 at all; Sonnet 5's $1.57 → $12.95 with the same collapse; Haiku 5.5's $0.18 → $1.12, with high already the step where TALLY-12 and Quarry Duel go to zero. The cheapest clean sweep in the grid is Opus 5.5 @ xhigh at $7.22 / 32 min. Below the last point, effort mostly bought nothing; on Haiku 4.5 it correlated with worse ledger scores (best sample 5/6 at low vs 2/6-band at high). Escalate one step when a config fails; buy the tier, not the grind, when you need clean.

F5 · Single-sample crowns die under replication

Three cc1 headliners regressed at n=2: Opus 4.8@low's perfect sweep (second ledger sample: 3/6 — its range is now 3–6 at every effort), Sonnet@xhigh's ledger crossing (5–6), and Haiku 4.5's crisp "effort inversions" (now wide variance bands: T3 2–5, T6 7–13, deep puzzles 3–6 at high). What replicated instead: Opus 5's cleanliness, the E generation wall, and Fable's stubborn single gauntlet point at every effort below max — a slip Fable 5.1 then reproduced at every effort below xhigh. Treat any n=1 surprise as a hypothesis; this repository exists so you can rerun it.

F6 · Cheap per token ≠ capable, but it is genuinely cheap

Haiku 4.5 was never clean and its variance bands are the widest in the grid — it scores 3–17 % on the three simulations at every effort — yet a full 13-task sweep costs $1.84–2.43, and inside the saturated zone it matched every tier in both samples. Haiku 5.5 replaces it at a tenth of the list price ($0.10/$0.50): the ten saturated tasks cost $0.04 at low and 3 minutes, every puzzle set, CSV, math and knowledge item is clean at every effort, and the simulations stop being noise — 86–89 % on TALLY-12 and a clean cold-chain pair at xhigh. What it cannot do is the ledger (3–6/6, never clean) or finish a 200-turn simulation inside the 128k output budget at high effort or above. Route mechanical work to Haiku 5.5 at low; route long exact simulation and precision aggregation elsewhere.

F7 · The bill is verbosity: effort multiplies cost for the same scores

Within every family, the 13-task sweep cost rises 1.3–8× from low to max (Haiku 4.5 $1.84→$2.43; Haiku 5.5 $0.18→$1.12; Sonnet 5 $1.57→$12.95; Sonnet 5.5 $1.69→$11.24≈; Opus 5 $5.24→$16.93; Opus 5.5 $3.20→$13.43; Fable 5 $11.19→$50.69; Fable 5.1 $10.42→$26.44) and time rises faster (Opus 5.5: 11→65 min; Sonnet 5.5: 13→150 min; Sonnet 5: 11→162 min; Haiku 5.5: 18→111 min) — above xhigh for identical scores, and on the Sonnet tier for worse ones. Opus 5.5 is the clearest case: max costs 1.9× its already-clean xhigh and takes twice as long for the same 13/13. Grind-prone precision work belongs at Opus 5.5's floor, or at Sonnet 5.5's if the work looks like the saturated ten — or give the model tools: in the archived tool-enabled rounds, execution access saturated these tasks for every tier.

F8 · The generation gap lives in novel semantics — and in reliability

Opus 4.8 stalls at 4/5 on the subtle semantic bugs at low and high and fixes the fifth only at xhigh — in all four samples across both study generations — while Opus 5 and Fable found 5/5 at every effort, twice. And at the same list price as Opus 5, Opus 4.8 never produced a clean sweep. The same-price generation upgrade is free reliability; take it and re-find your floor a step or two lower. The point releases replicated the pattern in miniature: Fable 5.1 kept 5/5 on the subtle bugs at every effort and moved Fable's clean point down one step (xhigh instead of max); Opus 5.5 also found 5/5 at every effort, but its cheaper list price is paid back in effort — it needs xhigh to be clean where Opus 5 needs medium. The Sonnet generation went the other way: Sonnet 5.5 finds 5/5 on the subtle bugs at every effort where Sonnet 5 stalled at 4/5 below high, and on the ten saturated tasks moves the Sonnet floor from max ($3.55) to xhigh ($1.29) at the same list price — though over all 13 tasks neither Sonnet is ever clean. The Haiku generation is the largest step in the grid: Haiku 5.5 costs a tenth of Haiku 4.5 per token, erases Haiku 4.5's variance bands on the saturated ten (puzzles, CSV, math and recall all clean at every effort), finds 5/5 subtle bugs at every effort where Haiku 4.5 wandered 3–5, and takes TALLY-12 from 3–6 % to 86–89 % at medium — while inheriting a new failure mode, the output cap, from high upward. Re-find the floor after every release, in both directions.

F9 · Three simulations, one real separator

TALLY-12 and Quarry Duel are held cleanly in both replicates by thirteen configurations each; cold-chain by nine: Opus 5.5 from high, Fable 5.1 from xhigh, Sonnet 5.5 at medium, xhigh and max, Haiku 5.5 at xhigh and max — and nobody else twice at any effort. Cold-chain is a 30-day, four-depot perishable-inventory simulation with ratio-based rationing; the failure is always one bookkeeping slip around a stockout or expiry day that propagates through every later checkpoint. Opus 5 lands at 100 % in one replicate and 20–50 % in the other at every effort; Opus 4.8 never exceeds 78 % except one perfect xhigh run; Fable 5 never holds it twice. It is the single task that separates the frontier, and it costs $0.04–$1.70 a run.

F10 · The effort axis shows, in both directions

Haiku 4.5 never leaves the noise floor on the simulations (3–17 % at every effort). Haiku 5.5 shows the whole axis in one family: at low it scores 25–52 % on TALLY-12, 0–10 % on cold-chain and declines Quarry Duel outright (prose instead of JSON, scored 0); at medium 86–89 %, 48–100 % and 25–44 %; from high its thinking consumes the 128k output budget on TALLY-12 and Quarry Duel (0 in every replicate) while cold-chain keeps improving to a clean pair at xhigh. Below medium every Sonnet collapses to the same noise (1–5 %); at low the Opus and Fable tiers still score 60–100 % on TALLY-12 and Quarry Duel but 35–50 % on cold-chain. Above medium the two Sonnets diverge: Sonnet 5.5 improves through xhigh (cold-chain clean, TALLY-12 85–90 %) and then falls off a cliff at max — 0 % on TALLY-12 in both replicates, because thinking consumes the 128k output budget and no answer arrives. Sonnet 5 peaks at medium (75–81 % everywhere) and degrades from high onwards, ending with two budget overruns at max. Max effort never improved a Sonnet or a Haiku on these tasks; it bought Opus 5.5 and Fable 5.1 nothing they did not already have at xhigh.

F11 · Replicates matter more on the simulations than anywhere else

The simulation graders award partial credit, but a single state slip cascades through everything after it, so the same configuration can score 30 % and 100 % on consecutive runs — Opus 5 at medium on Quarry Duel, Fable 5 at high on cold-chain, Haiku 5.5 at medium on cold-chain, Sonnet 5.5 at low on all three. The map's min–max cells carry that spread; treat any cell wider than 30 points of its total as "unreliable here", not as a mean, and read the scatter's circle size (mean share of points dropped) as the honest summary of a config's reliability.
