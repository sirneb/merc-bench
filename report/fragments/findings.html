Editorial synthesis of the graded data (charts and map regenerate automatically; this prose is curated). Every configuration now carries two full replicates through the reproducible runner; the retired first-generation dataset (a non-reproducible orchestration harness) is preserved in archive/workflow-runs/.

F1 · Most tasks saturate — then the cheapest config strictly wins

Knowledge recall, classic-bug review, state simulation, 5-attribute puzzles and regex writing were solved by essentially every configuration in every replicate; math, code trace, CSV and the constraint gauntlet sit within a one-or-two-point noise band for everything above Haiku. Same-score price spreads run 5–12×. For transforms, extraction, lookups, format work and classic review: buy the cheapest configuration that has passed the task for you.

F2 · Reliability floors, measured with replicates

Defining a config as clean only if it dropped zero points in every replicate of the 10 core tasks: Opus 5 is clean from medium up ($2.25, 9 min — the cheapest and nearly fastest clean config in the grid); Opus 5.5 from xhigh up ($2.55, 10 min); Fable 5.1 from xhigh up ($6.92, 16 min); Sonnet is clean only at max ($4.79, 33 min); Fable 5 only at max ($10.66, 27 min); Opus 4.8 and Haiku were never clean at any effort. Four of the ten replicated-clean configs in the entire grid belong to Opus 5; the two point releases added later (Fable 5.1, Opus 5.5) contribute two each.

F3 · Opus 5 is the value frontier, and replication strengthened it

Clean twice at medium, high, xhigh and max; at low it dropped exactly one gauntlet point — the same point — in both samples, while still solving the deep puzzles 6/6 and the subtle bugs 5/5 twice. Every other family got less reliable under replication; Opus 5 got confirmed. If you run one configuration for everything hard, Opus 5 @ medium is the measured answer. Opus 5.5, measured two months later at a lower list price ($4/$20), lands 30 cents behind it: clean at xhigh ($2.55, 10 min) and max, with one gauntlet point dropped in one replicate at medium and high and a ledger point (4–5/6) at low. Below its floor it is the cheapest and fastest thing in the grid above Haiku — a full low-effort sweep is $1.24 and 3 minutes — so the choice between the two Opus generations is "clean at medium for $2.25" versus "nearly clean at low for $1.24".

F4 · Effort buys the last point — at the wrong price

Replication revised our own earlier claim that max never wins: max is precisely where Sonnet and Fable first went zero-defect in both samples (Sonnet's xhigh and Fable's xhigh each still dropped a point in one replicate). But the last point is overpriced: Fable 5's ladder to its clean config runs $3.91→$10.66, Fable 5.1's $3.86→$6.92 (its slip clears one step earlier, at xhigh), Sonnet's $1.80→$4.79, and Opus 5.5's $1.24→$2.55 for xhigh and $7.00 for max — the steepest low→max multiplier in the grid (5.6×) for scores identical to xhigh. Opus 5 delivers replicated zero-defect at $2.25/9 min. Below the last point, effort mostly bought nothing; on Haiku it correlated with worse ledger scores (best sample 5/6 at low vs 2/6-band at high). Escalate one step when a config fails; buy the tier, not the grind, when you need clean.

F5 · Single-sample crowns die under replication

Three cc1 headliners regressed at n=2: Opus 4.8@low's perfect sweep (second ledger sample: 3/6 — its range is now 3–6 at every effort), Sonnet@xhigh's ledger crossing (5–6), and Haiku's crisp "effort inversions" (now wide variance bands: T3 2–5, T6 7–13, deep puzzles 3–6 at high). What replicated instead: Opus 5's cleanliness, the E generation wall, and Fable's stubborn single gauntlet point at every effort below max — a slip Fable 5.1 then reproduced at every effort below xhigh. Treat any n=1 surprise as a hypothesis; this repository exists so you can rerun it.

F6 · Cheap per token ≠ capable, but it is genuinely cheap

Haiku was never clean and its variance bands are the widest in the grid, yet a full 10-task sweep costs $0.86–1.14 — a fraction of the nearest floor — and inside the saturated zone it matched every tier in both samples. Route mechanical work there at low effort; never route precision aggregation or deep deduction there.

F7 · The bill is verbosity: effort multiplies cost for the same scores

Within every family, sweep cost rises 2–5.6× from low to max (Sonnet $1.80→$4.79; Opus 5 $2.00→$3.83; Opus 5.5 $1.24→$7.00; Fable 5 $3.91→$10.66; Fable 5.1 $3.86→$10.90) and time rises with it (Opus 5: 7→18 min; Opus 5.5: 3→33 min; Sonnet: 11→33 min) — almost always for identical scores. Opus 5.5 is the sharpest example: max costs 2.7× its already-clean xhigh and takes three times as long for the same 100/100. Grind-prone precision work belongs at Opus 5's floor — or give the model tools: in the archived tool-enabled rounds, execution access saturated these tasks for every tier.

F8 · The generation gap lives in novel semantics — and in reliability

Opus 4.8 stalls at 4/5 on the subtle semantic bugs at low and high and fixes the fifth only at xhigh — in all four samples across both study generations — while Opus 5 and Fable found 5/5 at every effort, twice. And at the same list price as Opus 5, Opus 4.8 never produced a clean sweep. The same-price generation upgrade is free reliability; take it and re-find your floor a step or two lower. The point releases replicated the pattern in miniature: Fable 5.1 kept 5/5 on the subtle bugs at every effort and moved Fable's clean point down one step (xhigh instead of max); Opus 5.5 also found 5/5 at every effort, but its cheaper list price is paid back in effort — it needs xhigh to be clean where Opus 5 needs medium. Re-find the floor after every release, in both directions.
