You are the dispatcher of a small freight rail network. Work through the four stages below EXACTLY as specified and report every stage's results. Each stage uses only the static data in this prompt and the results of the previous stage, so be precise: a wrong route changes the arrival time, which changes the dock queue, which changes the bills.

======================================================================
RULES
======================================================================

General
- Every shipment travels as its own train. Trains never interact on the track (no capacity limits, no meets).
- All timestamps are local depot time in one time zone with no daylight-saving change, written YYYY-MM-DDTHH:MM (24-hour clock). Every duration in this problem is a whole number of minutes, so no rounding is ever needed.
- Track segments are undirected (usable in either direction) with the km length listed.
- Station names are compared as plain strings; every station name starts with a different letter, so alphabetical order is simply the order of first letters.

Stage 1 - ROUTES
- A shipment's departure date is the date part of its ready timestamp.
- A maintenance-calendar entry '<date>  X -- Y' means segment X--Y is closed for the whole of that date: NO train whose departure date is that date may use that segment anywhere on its route, even if the train would only reach the segment on a later date. Closures listed for other dates are irrelevant to that train. Closures never delay a train; they only restrict which segments its route may use.
- The route is the simple path (no station visited twice) from origin to destination that minimises total km using only segments open on the departure date. Trains may pass through hub stations as intermediate stops.
- Tie-break: if two or more paths have the same minimum km, choose the one whose station sequence is alphabetically first, comparing station names position by position (the origin is the same for all, so the first differing position decides; e.g. [Ashford, Belmont, Kelso, Zeals] beats [Ashford, Corwen, Belmont, Zeals]).
- Report the full station sequence including origin and destination, and the total km.

Stage 2 - ARRIVALS
- The train departs the origin at exactly the ready timestamp. There is no dwell at the origin.
- Speed is exactly 60 km/h on every segment, so a segment of k km takes exactly k minutes. A segment is never interrupted once started.
- At every intermediate station (each station on the route except origin and destination) the train dwells 20 minutes. There is no dwell at the destination: the arrival time is the minute the train reaches the destination station.
- Crew rule: the crew keeps a driving counter that starts at 0 at the origin and increases by a segment's minutes when that segment is completed. Dwell and rest minutes do NOT count. Immediately before the train departs on any segment other than the first (i.e. after the 20-minute dwell), if the counter is 360 or more, the crew takes a 45-minute rest at that station (so that departure is delayed by 20+45 minutes in total) and the counter resets to 0. The check uses completed driving only: a segment is never split even if it pushes the counter far past 360. No rest is taken at the destination. A long trip may include more than one rest.
- Report the arrival timestamp and the number of rests taken.

Stage 3 - DOCKS
- Each destination hub has 2 identical unloading docks, dedicated to that hub. All docks are free from the start and operate around the clock. Only the shipments in this problem use them.
- Unloading a shipment takes ceil(tonnage / 25) x 30 minutes (e.g. 100 t -> 4 x 30 = 120 min; 101 t -> 5 x 30 = 150 min).
- Shipments bound for the same hub are served strictly in service order: earlier arrival first; if two arrive in the same minute, the smaller shipment id first. Process them one at a time in that order: take the dock that becomes free earliest (any free dock if several are idle); dock_start = the later of the shipment's arrival and that dock's free time; that dock is then busy until dock_start + unloading minutes, and is free again in that very minute (a new shipment may start in the same minute another finishes).
- wait_minutes = dock_start - arrival (in minutes).

Stage 4 - DEMURRAGE BILLING
- Per shipment: the first 90 minutes of wait are free. billable = max(0, wait_minutes - 90). blocks = ceil(billable / 30) (a started block counts in full). fee = blocks x $85; if the shipment is hazardous the fee is multiplied by 2; finally the fee is capped at $900 per shipment (the cap applies after the hazardous multiplier). All fees are whole dollars.
- Per customer: the sum of that customer's shipment fees (report every customer, including those with $0). Grand total: the sum over all shipments.
