§4Findings

Editorial synthesis of the graded data (charts and map above regenerate automatically; this prose is curated — see §8 for the correction history).

F1 · Most tasks saturate — then the cheapest config strictly wins

Eight of twelve tasks (math, code trace above Haiku, 5-attribute puzzles, CSV normalization, knowledge recall, regex writing, classic-bug review, state simulation — and, after the grading correction, the constraint gauntlet) are solved by essentially every configuration. Same-score price spreads run 5–30×. For transforms, extraction, lookups, format work and classic review: buy the cheapest configuration that has ever passed the task for you.

F2 · The walls are precision walls, and they set each tier's effort floor

Three tasks separate hard: the ledger audit (exact aggregation over 1,400 rows), the minimal-clue logic puzzles, and the subtle semantic bugs. They define effort floors, not ceilings: Sonnet crosses the ledger only at xhigh — via a 256,000-token, $4.60, 29-minute grind on that one answer — while Opus 5 crosses at medium ($1.10, 4 min) and Fable at low. Haiku never crosses. Walls track frontier tier, not generation (Sonnet 5 shares Opus 5's generation and needs three more effort steps): what tier buys is a cheap, fast floor.

F3 · "Big model, low effort" is the value pattern — with a variance tax

Fable@low swept everything in its sample, fastest overall. Opus 5@low crossed both knowledge walls at half Fable's price but dropped ~1 point per 10-task sweep in an unpredictable place (n=3); one effort step to medium bought back a perfect, replicated sweep at ≈$2.50/8 min — the cheapest perfection in the dataset.

F4 · Effort climbs to the floor, then only burns

Above each tier's floor, no added effort improved any score in this dataset. Above-floor sweeps cost 1.2–2.6× their tier's floor for identical results. Max effort never won once, and destroyed value twice: Sonnet@max spent 512k tokens/$8.44/57 min on the ledger before exceeding the model's own 128k-per-response output ceiling (that cell cannot complete at any setting), and Opus 5@max regressed the constraint gauntlet in one of two samples. Escalate exactly one step when a configuration fails; never buy max.

F5 · Reliability at the top is model-specific

On the six-puzzle set, mid-effort Fable failed the same puzzle by the same two cells in 2 of 4 attempts (a last-mile verification skip — it also spent the fewest top-tier tokens there); Sonnet@low, Opus 4.8@high and Opus 5 were perfect on every attempt (Opus 5: 8 for 8). Don't assume the priciest model at its default is the safest; push Fable to xhigh for quick verification-heavy logic, or use the tiers that measured stable.

F6 · Cheap per token ≠ cheap per task ≠ fast

At its capability edge Haiku grinds long and still fails: 6.5× the cost per solved puzzle of Sonnet@low, and the longest wall-clock in several tasks despite the lowest sticker price. Route to Haiku only inside the saturated zone.

F7 · Token volume is the bill; walls tax the smallest capable model hardest

The ledger's effort ladder on Sonnet is the definitive verbosity curve: 13.7k → 58.6k → 186.8k → 256k → 512k output tokens as effort rises, with exactly one score improvement along the way and a ceiling-death at the end. Crossing a wall by effort on a mid tier costs more than jumping tiers ($4.60/29 min vs $1.10/4 min for the same answer). Harnesses that limit response length will convert the biggest grinds into hard failures — keep grind-prone precision work at the frontier tier's floor, or give the model tools (tool access saturates these tasks entirely).

F8 · Same-price generation upgrades are free quality

Opus 5 matches or beats Opus 4.8 at the same list price on every task measured, usually at a lower effort setting (subtle bugs: 5/5 at low vs 4/5 at high; ledger floor: medium vs xhigh). Take same-price upgrades immediately and re-find your floor one or two effort steps lower than before.

§5The tasks

One card per task: design, rationale, and verdict. Full prompts, schemas and self-contained graders live in tasks/.

Subtle semantic bugs (E)

Design5 planted conceptual bugs in a search index

WhyNovel semantic defects (document-frequency semantics, operator precedence, case asymmetry, phrase adjacency, normalization denominator) that resist pattern-matching review.

FoundThe knowledge wall: 4.x-generation models stall at 4/5 at high effort (xhigh fixes them); 5th-generation frontier finds 5/5 even at low effort for a tenth of the escalation cost.

Mental math (T1)

Design16 exact computations, no tools

WhyTests exact arithmetic under load: 4-digit products, signed sums, modular exponentiation, compound percentages. Hypothesis: tier gaps and effort gains on raw computation.

FoundNear-saturated: only Haiku@high missed one item (a mod-pow). Effort spent up to 4.3x tokens for identical scores. 2026 frontier and mid-tier models simply do arithmetic.

Code trace (T2)

Designpredict exact stdout of 8 Python snippets

WhyPython semantics traps (mutable defaults, closure late binding, dict key collapse, try/finally returns, generator exhaustion). Ground truth produced by executing the snippets.

FoundWalls Haiku only (6/8 at high, 7/8 at low). Everything Sonnet@low and up is perfect, with rare one-point slips at frontier low effort.

Ledger audit (T3)

Design6 exact aggregates over 1,400 transaction rows, no tools

WhyPrecision aggregation at scale: balances, filtered counts, running-threshold detection, maxima, group sums, busiest-day counts. The strongest separator in the study.

FoundThe wall battery: scores 3/6 to 6/6 by config; effort floors differ per tier (Sonnet crosses only at xhigh via a 256k-token grind; Opus 5 at medium; Fable at low). Verbosity events and output-ceiling failures live here.

Constraint gauntlet (T4)

Designone essay satisfying 10 mechanical constraints

WhyInstruction-following under load: exact word counts, an e-free paragraph, exact substring counts, ordered first words, length caps -- all machine-checkable.

FoundThe cleanest effort ladder: Sonnet 3/10 at low to 10/10 at high. Haiku walls (2-8/10). Frontier low effort slips here in some samples; max regressed once (the e-free rule).

Logic puzzles (5-attribute) (T5A)

Design3 zebra puzzles, 25 cells each, solver-verified unique, no tools

WhyDeduction with a bigger grid. Hypothesis: scale deepens the reasoning wall.

FoundHypothesis rejected -- saturated for every config (richer clue sets propagate more easily; difficulty is clue minimality, not grid size). One frontier-low sample dropped 2 cells, the only miss recorded.

Logic puzzles (4-attribute, minimal clues) (T5B)

Design6 zebra puzzles with minimized clue sets, no tools

WhyPure deduction with irreducible clue sets. Used for reliability replicates: same set, repeated attempts per config.

FoundSeparates hard: Haiku 2/6; Sonnet@low perfect on all attempts; mid-effort Fable failed the same puzzle by the same 2 cells in 2 of 4 attempts (a last-mile verification skip); Opus 5 clean on 8 attempts.

Strict CSV normalization (T6)

Designmessy records to exact canonical CSV under 9 interacting rules

WhyAttention-to-detail transform: date normalization, dedupe-by-latest, conditional quoting, multi-key sort, invalid-row filtering. Exact line-by-line grading.

FoundSaturated at every tier and effort (rare frontier-low one-line slips). Rules-following transforms are safe on the cheapest configs.

Knowledge recall (T7)

Design20 unambiguous technical facts, no tools or web

WhyParametric knowledge floor: chemistry symbols, ports, encodings, RFCs, Roman numerals. Hypothesis: bigger models know more.

FoundFully saturated -- 20/20 for every config and sample. Common technical knowledge has no tier gradient in 2026.

Regex writing without testing (T8)

Design8 fullmatch patterns against trap sets, graded by execution

WhyWrite-only correctness: the model cannot test its patterns; the grader executes them against must-match / must-not-match sets.

FoundSaturated on score (8/8 everywhere) with a large efficiency tell: Haiku took 20x the wall-clock of frontier-low for the same result.

Bug review without tools (T9)

Design7 planted classic bugs in a rate limiter, pure code review

WhyReview with no execution: boundary conditions, off-by-ones, mutable default sharing, clamp ordering, fractional truncation. Behavioral grading of the returned fixed module.

FoundSaturated -- every config found all 7. Classic-pattern bugs are within every tier's reach; only novel semantic bugs (see e-subtle-bugs) separate.

State simulation (T10)

Design30 sequential warehouse-robot commands, exact final state

WhySustained state tracking with conflict rules (walls, collisions, capacity, no-ops). Simulator-generated ground truth.

FoundSaturated for every config on exact state (two mid-effort samples dropped robot cells). Short-horizon state tracking is solved.

§8Method corrections & honest limits

Corrections

This dataset supersedes two earlier grading decisions, both found by re-auditing and both correctable by anyone from the shipped raw answers: (1) constraint-gauntlet (T4) scores were initially under-graded for runs that returned JSON-wrapped text — the wrapper was scored as content, producing an apparent "effort ladder" (3/10→10/10 on Sonnet) that disappears when the unwrapped essays are graded; T4 is in fact near-saturated for every tier above Haiku. (2) A small number of answer-format deviations (prose instead of schema JSON) are parsed tolerantly and graded on content, with format non-compliance noted rather than punished. The graders in tasks/ implement the corrected policy; make verify reproduces every published number from raw answers.

Limits

Most cells are n=1; frontier configurations carry n≤3 plus repeated puzzle attempts — treat single-cell gaps as directional and cross-task patterns as the findings. Tasks are synthetic, single-shot, ≤60 minutes, without repository context; long-horizon autonomy and agentic orchestration are not measured here, which is exactly where the priciest configurations claim their value. A minority of records lack usage data (a logging fault in two replicate batches); their costs are null and excluded from totals, and totals containing estimated cells are marked ≈. Costs use standard list prices (Sonnet's introductory pricing would flatten ~⅓ off its rows through 2026-08-31). All original runs came from one account over three days via Claude Code subagents; serving stacks change — rerun before trusting fine margins.
