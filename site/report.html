§2How this was measured

ControlHow
Reproducible harnessEvery active record came through the shipped runner (runner/run.py, Claude Code headless path) with answer validation and up to 3 corrective retries; transport failures are quarantined as invalid, never scored. One command reruns any cell; runner/sweep.py reruns the whole grid.
Ground truth firstReference implementations cross-checked against independently-written oracles and fuzzing (which caught two harness bugs before any model ran); solver-verified unique puzzles; executed code snippets; generator-computed ledger and simulation answers. All ground truth ships in tasks/*/.

Grading recomputedNothing is trusted from stored records except raw answers. runner/aggregate.py regrades every answer through the shipped graders on every build; make verify is the audit path.
Cost accountingPer-run dollars (input + cache writes + cache reads + output, including retries) from the CLI's per-run usage report, priced at standard list rates. Subscription serving — costs are imputed, not billed.
No toolsTasks forbid tool use (tool access lets every tier self-verify to perfection, masking differences — measured in the archived tool-enabled rounds).

§4Findings

Editorial synthesis of the graded data (charts and map regenerate automatically; this prose is curated). Every configuration now carries two full replicates through the reproducible runner; the retired first-generation dataset (a non-reproducible orchestration harness) is preserved in archive/workflow-runs/.

F1 · Most tasks saturate — then the cheapest config strictly wins

Knowledge recall, classic-bug review, state simulation, 5-attribute puzzles and regex writing were solved by essentially every configuration in every replicate; math, code trace, CSV and the constraint gauntlet sit within a one-or-two-point noise band for everything above Haiku. Same-score price spreads run 5–12×. For transforms, extraction, lookups, format work and classic review: buy the cheapest configuration that has passed the task for you.

F2 · Reliability floors, measured with replicates

Defining a config as clean only if it dropped zero points in every replicate of the 10 core tasks: Opus 5 is clean from medium up ($2.25, 9 min — the cheapest and nearly fastest clean config in the grid); Sonnet is clean only at max ($4.79, 33 min); Fable only at max ($10.66, 27 min); Opus 4.8 and Haiku were never clean at any effort. Four of the seven replicated-clean configs in the entire grid belong to Opus 5.

F3 · Opus 5 is the value frontier, and replication strengthened it

Clean twice at medium, high, xhigh and max; at low it dropped exactly one gauntlet point — the same point — in both samples, while still solving the deep puzzles 6/6 and the subtle bugs 5/5 twice. Every other family got less reliable under replication; Opus 5 got confirmed. If you run one configuration for everything hard, Opus 5 @ medium is the measured answer.

F4 · Effort buys the last point — at the wrong price

Replication revised our own earlier claim that max never wins: max is precisely where Sonnet and Fable first went zero-defect in both samples (Sonnet's xhigh and Fable's xhigh each still dropped a point in one replicate). But the last point is overpriced: Fable's ladder to its clean config runs $3.91→$10.66 and Sonnet's $1.80→$4.79, while Opus 5 delivers replicated zero-defect at $2.25/9 min. Below the last point, effort mostly bought nothing; on Haiku it correlated with worse ledger scores (best sample 5/6 at low vs 2/6-band at high). Escalate one step when a config fails; buy the tier, not the grind, when you need clean.

F5 · Single-sample crowns die under replication

Three cc1 headliners regressed at n=2: Opus 4.8@low's perfect sweep (second ledger sample: 3/6 — its range is now 3–6 at every effort), Sonnet@xhigh's ledger crossing (5–6), and Haiku's crisp "effort inversions" (now wide variance bands: T3 2–5, T6 7–13, deep puzzles 3–6 at high). What replicated instead: Opus 5's cleanliness, the E generation wall, and Fable's stubborn single gauntlet point at every effort below max. Treat any n=1 surprise as a hypothesis; this repository exists so you can rerun it.

F6 · Cheap per token ≠ capable, but it is genuinely cheap

Haiku was never clean and its variance bands are the widest in the grid, yet a full 10-task sweep costs $0.86–1.14 — a fraction of the nearest floor — and inside the saturated zone it matched every tier in both samples. Route mechanical work there at low effort; never route precision aggregation or deep deduction there.

F7 · The bill is verbosity: effort multiplies cost for the same scores

Within every family, sweep cost rises 2–2.7× from low to max (Sonnet $1.80→$4.79; Opus 5 $2.00→$3.83; Fable $3.91→$10.66) and time rises with it (Opus 5: 7→18 min; Sonnet: 11→33 min) — almost always for identical scores. Grind-prone precision work belongs at Opus 5's floor — or give the model tools: in the archived tool-enabled rounds, execution access saturated these tasks for every tier.

F8 · The generation gap lives in novel semantics — and in reliability

Opus 4.8 stalls at 4/5 on the subtle semantic bugs at low and high and fixes the fifth only at xhigh — in all four samples across both study generations — while Opus 5 and Fable found 5/5 at every effort, twice. And at the same list price as Opus 5, Opus 4.8 never produced a clean sweep. The same-price generation upgrade is free reliability; take it and re-find your floor a step or two lower.

§5The tasks

One card per task: design, rationale, and verdict. Full prompts, schemas and self-contained graders live in tasks/.

Subtle semantic bugs (E)

Design5 planted conceptual bugs in a search index

WhyNovel semantic defects (document-frequency semantics, operator precedence, case asymmetry, phrase adjacency, normalization denominator) that resist pattern-matching review.

FoundThe generation wall: Opus 4.8 stalls at 4/5 below xhigh in both study generations; Opus 5 and Fable find 5/5 at every effort. Sonnet crosses at high; Haiku is noisy (3-5/5).

Mental math (T1)

Design16 exact computations, no tools

WhyTests exact arithmetic under load: 4-digit products, signed sums, modular exponentiation, compound percentages. Hypothesis: tier gaps and effort gains on raw computation.

FoundNear-saturated: only Haiku drops an item (n=1 cells). Effort spends multiples of tokens for identical scores. 2026 models simply do arithmetic.

Code trace (T2)

Designpredict exact stdout of 8 Python snippets

WhyPython semantics traps (mutable defaults, closure late binding, dict key collapse, try/finally returns, generator exhaustion). Ground truth produced by executing the snippets.

FoundA one-point noise band: scattered single misses across Haiku (all efforts), Sonnet low/medium and Opus 4.8@xhigh; perfect elsewhere. No config missed two.

Ledger audit (T3)

Design6 exact aggregates over 1,400 transaction rows, no tools

WhyPrecision aggregation at scale: balances, filtered counts, running-threshold detection, maxima, group sums, busiest-day counts. The strongest separator in the study.

FoundThe wall battery — the dataset's lone hard separator, and the noisiest cell in the grid: Opus 4.8 spans 3-6/6 across replicates at every effort, Haiku 2-5/6. Only Opus 5 (medium up), Sonnet@max and Fable@max held 6/6 in both replicates. The biggest token grinds live here.

Constraint gauntlet (T4)

Designone essay satisfying 10 mechanical constraints

WhyInstruction-following under load: exact word counts, an e-free paragraph, exact substring counts, ordered first words, length caps -- all machine-checkable.

FoundNear-saturated (a published correction: the original "Sonnet 3/10 to 10/10 effort ladder" was a grading artifact from scoring JSON-wrapped text). Every tier above Haiku scores 9-10/10; Fable persistently dropped the same single constraint at every effort below max, in both replicates; Haiku drops 1-2.

Logic puzzles (5-attribute) (T5A)

Design3 zebra puzzles, 25 cells each, solver-verified unique, no tools

WhyDeduction with a bigger grid. Hypothesis: scale deepens the reasoning wall.

FoundHypothesis rejected -- saturated for every config above Haiku (richer clue sets propagate more easily; difficulty is clue minimality, not grid size). Haiku@medium dropped cells on two puzzles, the only miss recorded.

Logic puzzles (4-attribute, minimal clues) (T5B)

Design6 zebra puzzles with minimized clue sets, no tools

WhyPure deduction with irreducible clue sets. Used for reliability replicates: same set, repeated attempts per config.

FoundSeparates Haiku from everyone: every non-Haiku config solved all 6 in both replicates (one Fable@low sample: 5/6). Haiku@high spans 3-6/6 across samples — a wide variance band where the first generation of this study saw a crisp collapse.

Strict CSV normalization (T6)

Designmessy records to exact canonical CSV under 9 interacting rules

WhyAttention-to-detail transform: date normalization, dedupe-by-latest, conditional quoting, multi-key sort, invalid-row filtering. Exact line-by-line grading.

FoundSaturated at every tier and effort (rare frontier-low one-line slips). Rules-following transforms are safe on the cheapest configs.

Knowledge recall (T7)

Design20 unambiguous technical facts, no tools or web

WhyParametric knowledge floor: chemistry symbols, ports, encodings, RFCs, Roman numerals. Hypothesis: bigger models know more.

FoundFully saturated -- 20/20 for every config and sample. Common technical knowledge has no tier gradient in 2026.

Regex writing without testing (T8)

Design8 fullmatch patterns against trap sets, graded by execution

WhyWrite-only correctness: the model cannot test its patterns; the grader executes them against must-match / must-not-match sets.

FoundSaturated on score (8/8 everywhere, one Haiku@low 7/8) with a large efficiency tell: cost and wall-clock vary by an order of magnitude for the same result.

Bug review without tools (T9)

Design7 planted classic bugs in a rate limiter, pure code review

WhyReview with no execution: boundary conditions, off-by-ones, mutable default sharing, clamp ordering, fractional truncation. Behavioral grading of the returned fixed module.

FoundSaturated -- every config found all 7. Classic-pattern bugs are within every tier's reach; only novel semantic bugs (see e-subtle-bugs) separate.

State simulation (T10)

Design30 sequential warehouse-robot commands, exact final state

WhySustained state tracking with conflict rules (walls, collisions, capacity, no-ops). Simulator-generated ground truth.

FoundSaturated for every config on exact state (two mid-effort samples dropped robot cells). Short-horizon state tracking is solved.

§8Method corrections & honest limits

Dataset generations & corrections

This is the study's second-generation dataset at two full replicates per configuration: every active record came through the shipped, reproducible runner (Claude Code headless path with answer validation and retry), and each config's 12 tasks were run twice (sample tags cc1/cc2; a few haiku@medium cells carry a third early sample). The first-generation records (a session-bound orchestration harness) are preserved in archive/workflow-runs/. Replication materially revised three of our own published claims — Opus 4.8@low's perfect sweep, Sonnet@xhigh's ledger crossing, and "max effort never wins" — all documented in F4/F5. Two grading corrections carry forward: JSON-wrapped answers are graded on content, and transport failures are quarantined as invalid rather than scored.

Limits

n=2 is still small — ranges are honest but confidence intervals are not computable; the plan is to accumulate replicates over time (one runner/sweep.py --sample auto per pass). All runs came from one account over three days on subscription serving; costs are imputed from measured tokens at standard list prices. Tasks are synthetic, single-shot, ≤35 minutes, without repository context; long-horizon autonomy and agentic orchestration are not measured. Response-length headroom was raised to the models' own output ceilings; harnesses with tighter caps will fail the biggest grinds. Usage-window exhaustion during long sweeps produces transport failures — the sweep driver cleans and refills them automatically.
