§1The bottom line

Ten tasks saturate, three decide
ten of the thirteen core tasks are solved by every tier above Haiku at nearly every effort — there, the cheapest configuration strictly wins. The three long simulations (T13 TALLY-12, T14 cold-chain, T15 Quarry Duel) are where the frontier separates.
The reliability floor
cheapest config clean in every replicate of all 13 core tasks: Opus 5.5 xhigh ($7.22/32m) · Fable 5.1 xhigh ($19.61/48m). Only two families ever get there — Opus 5.5 from xhigh and Fable 5.1 from xhigh, at 2.7× the price. Haiku, Sonnet 5, Sonnet 5.5, Opus 4.8, Opus 5 and Fable 5 are never clean at any effort; cold-chain (T14) is what stops almost all of them.
Effort has a ceiling
below the floor, a config loses one or two stubborn points on the ten saturated tasks and a cascade on the simulations; above xhigh, max buys nothing it did not already have (Opus 5.5 $7.22→$13.43, Fable 5.1 $19.61→$26.44 for identical scores) and on the Sonnet tier it overruns the output budget and scores zero. Pick the first clean step; never max.

§2How this was measured

ControlHow
Reproducible harnessEvery active record came through the shipped runner (runner/run.py, Claude Code headless path) with answer validation and up to 3 corrective retries; transport failures are quarantined as invalid, never scored. One command reruns any cell; runner/sweep.py reruns the whole grid.
Ground truth firstReference implementations cross-checked against independently-written oracles and fuzzing (which caught two harness bugs before any model ran); solver-verified unique puzzles; executed code snippets; generator-computed ledger and simulation answers. All ground truth ships in tasks/*/.
One variable at a timeIdentical prompts per task across every configuration; only the model or its effort setting changes. Prompts ship verbatim in tasks/*/prompt.txt.
Grading recomputedNothing is trusted from stored records except raw answers. runner/aggregate.py regrades every answer through the shipped graders on every build; make verify is the audit path.
Cost accountingPer-run dollars (input + cache writes + cache reads + output, including retries) from the CLI's per-run usage report, priced at standard list rates, with cache writes at the harness's TTL rate (1-hour, 2× input, under Claude Code) and every price row verified against the CLI's list-basis cost fields. Costs are recomputed from stored token usage on every build. Subscription serving — costs are imputed, not billed.
No toolsTasks forbid tool use (tool access lets every tier self-verify to perfection, masking differences — measured in the archived tool-enabled rounds).

§4Findings

Editorial synthesis of the graded data (charts and map regenerate automatically; this prose is curated). Every configuration now carries two full replicates through the reproducible runner; the retired first-generation dataset (a non-reproducible orchestration harness) is preserved in archive/workflow-runs/.

F1 · Most tasks saturate — then the cheapest config strictly wins

Knowledge recall, classic-bug review, state simulation, 5-attribute puzzles and regex writing were solved by essentially every configuration in every replicate; math, code trace, CSV and the constraint gauntlet sit within a one-or-two-point noise band for everything above Haiku. Same-score price spreads run 5–12×. For transforms, extraction, lookups, format work and classic review: buy the cheapest configuration that has passed the task for you.

F2 · Reliability floors, measured with replicates

Defining a config as clean only if it dropped zero points in every replicate of all 13 core tasks, exactly four configurations in the grid qualify: Opus 5.5 at xhigh ($7.22, 32 min) and max ($13.43, 65 min), and Fable 5.1 at xhigh ($19.61, 48 min) and max ($26.44, 67 min). Nobody else is ever clean. The barrier is almost always cold-chain (T14): Opus 5 holds it in one replicate out of two at every effort, Fable 5 never holds it twice, Sonnet 5.5 holds it at medium, xhigh and max but then drops TALLY-12 or Quarry Duel, and Opus 4.8 never exceeds 78 % on it except once. On the ten original tasks alone the picture was far flatter — Sonnet 5.5 @ xhigh ($1.29), Opus 5 @ medium ($2.76), Opus 5.5 @ xhigh and Fable 5.1 @ xhigh were all "clean" — which is precisely why those three simulations were added: they turn single-point noise into a ranking.

F3 · Opus 5.5 is the value frontier; Opus 5 and Sonnet 5.5 are the value frontier of the saturated ten

Opus 5.5 is clean on all 13 tasks twice at xhigh and max, and at high it misses only one gauntlet point in one replicate while holding all three simulations ($4.77, 19 min) — the cheapest "nearly clean" config in the grid by a wide margin. Fable 5.1 reaches the same place at xhigh for 2.7× the money and 1.5× the time. Below them the ordering is by what each model cannot hold: Opus 5 is clean on the ten saturated tasks from medium and on TALLY-12 and Quarry Duel from xhigh, but cold-chain is a coin flip at every effort; Sonnet 5.5 is clean on the saturated ten from xhigh for $1.29 and on cold-chain from medium, but never holds Quarry Duel and stops answering TALLY-12 at max; Fable 5 clears everything but cold-chain at xhigh; Sonnet 5 peaks at medium and degrades. If you run one configuration for everything, Opus 5.5 @ xhigh is the measured answer; if your work looks like the saturated ten, Sonnet 5.5 @ xhigh at a sixth of the price. Opus 5.5, measured two months later at a lower list price ($4/$20), lands 32 cents behind it: clean at xhigh ($3.08, 10 min) and max, with one gauntlet point dropped in one replicate at medium and high and a ledger point (4–5/6) at low. Below its floor it is the fastest thing in the grid — a full low-effort sweep is 3 minutes and $1.78, a quarter less than Opus 5@low — so the choice between the two Opus generations is "clean at medium for $2.76" versus "nearly clean at low for $1.78".

F4 · Effort buys the last point — at the wrong price

Replication revised our own earlier claim that max never wins: on the ten saturated tasks, max is precisely where Sonnet 5 and Fable 5 first went zero-defect in both samples (Sonnet 5's xhigh and Fable 5's xhigh each still dropped a point in one replicate). The hard tier then revised it back for the Sonnet tier: at max both Sonnets overrun the output or wall-clock budget and score zero on tasks they partly solve at medium (H3). But the last point is overpriced: over the 13 tasks Opus 5.5's ladder runs $3.20 (low) → $7.22 (xhigh, clean) → $13.43 (max, still clean, nothing gained); Fable 5.1's $10.42 → $19.61 (xhigh, clean) → $26.44; Fable 5's $11.19 → $50.69 at max without ever going clean; Sonnet 5.5's $1.69 → $11.24, with max the step where it stops answering TALLY-12 at all; Sonnet 5's $1.57 → $7.68 with the same collapse. The cheapest clean sweep in the grid is Opus 5.5 @ xhigh at $7.22 / 32 min. Below the last point, effort mostly bought nothing; on Haiku it correlated with worse ledger scores (best sample 5/6 at low vs 2/6-band at high). Escalate one step when a config fails; buy the tier, not the grind, when you need clean.

F5 · Single-sample crowns die under replication

Three cc1 headliners regressed at n=2: Opus 4.8@low's perfect sweep (second ledger sample: 3/6 — its range is now 3–6 at every effort), Sonnet@xhigh's ledger crossing (5–6), and Haiku's crisp "effort inversions" (now wide variance bands: T3 2–5, T6 7–13, deep puzzles 3–6 at high). What replicated instead: Opus 5's cleanliness, the E generation wall, and Fable's stubborn single gauntlet point at every effort below max — a slip Fable 5.1 then reproduced at every effort below xhigh. Treat any n=1 surprise as a hypothesis; this repository exists so you can rerun it.

F6 · Cheap per token ≠ capable, but it is genuinely cheap

Haiku was never clean and its variance bands are the widest in the grid — it scores 3–17 % on the three simulations at every effort — yet a full 13-task sweep costs $1.84–2.43, a quarter of the nearest floor, and inside the saturated zone it matched every tier in both samples. Route mechanical work there at low effort; never route precision aggregation or deep deduction there.

F7 · The bill is verbosity: effort multiplies cost for the same scores

Within every family, the 13-task sweep cost rises 1.3–6.7× from low to max (Haiku $1.84→$2.43; Sonnet 5 $1.57→$7.68; Sonnet 5.5 $1.69→$11.24; Opus 5 $5.24→$16.93; Opus 5.5 $3.20→$13.43; Fable 5 $11.19→$50.69; Fable 5.1 $10.42→$26.44) and time rises faster (Opus 5.5: 11→65 min; Sonnet 5.5: 13→150 min; Sonnet 5: 11→162 min) — above xhigh for identical scores, and on the Sonnet tier for worse ones. Opus 5.5 is the clearest case: max costs 1.9× its already-clean xhigh and takes twice as long for the same 13/13. Grind-prone precision work belongs at Opus 5.5's floor, or at Sonnet 5.5's if the work looks like the saturated ten — or give the model tools: in the archived tool-enabled rounds, execution access saturated these tasks for every tier.

F8 · The generation gap lives in novel semantics — and in reliability

Opus 4.8 stalls at 4/5 on the subtle semantic bugs at low and high and fixes the fifth only at xhigh — in all four samples across both study generations — while Opus 5 and Fable found 5/5 at every effort, twice. And at the same list price as Opus 5, Opus 4.8 never produced a clean sweep. The same-price generation upgrade is free reliability; take it and re-find your floor a step or two lower. The point releases replicated the pattern in miniature: Fable 5.1 kept 5/5 on the subtle bugs at every effort and moved Fable's clean point down one step (xhigh instead of max); Opus 5.5 also found 5/5 at every effort, but its cheaper list price is paid back in effort — it needs xhigh to be clean where Opus 5 needs medium. The Sonnet generation went the other way: Sonnet 5.5 finds 5/5 on the subtle bugs at every effort where Sonnet 5 stalled at 4/5 below high, and on the ten saturated tasks moves the Sonnet floor from max ($3.55) to xhigh ($1.29) at the same list price — though over all 13 tasks neither Sonnet is ever clean. Re-find the floor after every release, in both directions.

F9 · Three simulations, one real separator

TALLY-12 and Quarry Duel are held cleanly in both replicates by thirteen configurations each; cold-chain by seven: Opus 5.5 from high, Fable 5.1 from xhigh, Sonnet 5.5 at medium, xhigh and max — and nobody else twice at any effort. Cold-chain is a 30-day, four-depot perishable-inventory simulation with ratio-based rationing; the failure is always one bookkeeping slip around a stockout or expiry day that propagates through every later checkpoint. Opus 5 lands at 100 % in one replicate and 20–50 % in the other at every effort; Opus 4.8 never exceeds 78 % except one perfect xhigh run; Fable 5 never holds it twice. It is the single task that separates the frontier, and it costs $0.20–$1.70 a run.

F10 · The effort axis shows, in both directions

Haiku never leaves the noise floor on the simulations (3–17 % at every effort). Below medium every Sonnet collapses to the same noise (1–5 %); at low the Opus and Fable tiers still score 60–100 % on TALLY-12 and Quarry Duel but 35–50 % on cold-chain. Above medium the two Sonnets diverge: Sonnet 5.5 improves through xhigh (cold-chain clean, TALLY-12 85–90 %) and then falls off a cliff at max — 0 % on TALLY-12 in both replicates, because thinking consumes the 128k output budget and no answer arrives. Sonnet 5 peaks at medium (75–81 % everywhere) and degrades from high onwards, ending with two budget overruns at max. Max effort never improved a Sonnet on these tasks; it bought Opus 5.5 and Fable 5.1 nothing they did not already have at xhigh.

F11 · Replicates matter more on the simulations than anywhere else

The simulation graders award partial credit, but a single state slip cascades through everything after it, so the same configuration can score 30 % and 100 % on consecutive runs — Opus 5 at medium on Quarry Duel, Fable 5 at high on cold-chain, Sonnet 5.5 at low on all three. The map's min–max cells carry that spread; treat any cell wider than 30 points of its total as "unreliable here", not as a mean, and read the scatter's circle size (mean share of points dropped) as the honest summary of a config's reliability.

§5The tasks

One card per task: design, rationale, and verdict. Full prompts, schemas and self-contained graders live in tasks/.

Subtle semantic bugs (E)

Design5 planted conceptual bugs in a search index

WhyNovel semantic defects (document-frequency semantics, operator precedence, case asymmetry, phrase adjacency, normalization denominator) that resist pattern-matching review.

FoundThe generation wall: Opus 4.8 stalls at 4/5 below xhigh in both study generations; Opus 5, Opus 5.5, Sonnet 5.5, Fable 5 and Fable 5.1 find 5/5 at every effort. Sonnet 5 crosses only at high; Haiku is noisy (3-5/5).

Mental math (T1)

Design16 exact computations, no tools

WhyTests exact arithmetic under load: 4-digit products, signed sums, modular exponentiation, compound percentages. Hypothesis: tier gaps and effort gains on raw computation.

FoundNear-saturated: only Haiku drops an item (n=1 cells). Effort spends multiples of tokens for identical scores. 2026 models simply do arithmetic.

Code trace (T2)

Designpredict exact stdout of 8 Python snippets

WhyPython semantics traps (mutable defaults, closure late binding, dict key collapse, try/finally returns, generator exhaustion). Ground truth produced by executing the snippets.

FoundA one-point noise band: scattered single misses across Haiku (all efforts), Sonnet 5 low/medium and Opus 4.8@xhigh; perfect elsewhere, Sonnet 5.5 included. No config missed two.

Ledger audit (T3)

Design6 exact aggregates over 1,400 transaction rows, no tools

WhyPrecision aggregation at scale: balances, filtered counts, running-threshold detection, maxima, group sums, busiest-day counts. The strongest separator in the study.

FoundThe wall battery — the dataset's lone hard separator, and the noisiest cell in the grid: Opus 4.8 spans 3-6/6 across replicates at every effort, Haiku 2-5/6. Only Opus 5 (medium up), Opus 5.5 (medium up), Sonnet 5.5 (high up), Fable 5.1 (every effort), Sonnet 5@max and Fable 5@max held 6/6 in both replicates; Opus 5.5@low dropped one (4-5/6), Sonnet 5.5 dropped one at low and one or two at medium. The biggest token grinds live here.

Constraint gauntlet (T4)

Designone essay satisfying 10 mechanical constraints

WhyInstruction-following under load: exact word counts, an e-free paragraph, exact substring counts, ordered first words, length caps -- all machine-checkable.

FoundNear-saturated (a published correction: the original "Sonnet 3/10 to 10/10 effort ladder" was a grading artifact from scoring JSON-wrapped text). Every tier above Haiku scores 9-10/10; Fable 5 persistently dropped the same single constraint at every effort below max, in both replicates; Fable 5.1 does the same below xhigh; Opus 5.5 dropped it in one replicate at medium and high (clean at low, xhigh and max); Sonnet 5.5 dropped it in both replicates at low and once at high; Sonnet 5 never dropped it; Haiku drops 1-2.

Logic puzzles (5-attribute) (T5A)

Design3 zebra puzzles, 25 cells each, solver-verified unique, no tools

WhyDeduction with a bigger grid. Hypothesis: scale deepens the reasoning wall.

FoundHypothesis rejected -- saturated for every config above Haiku (richer clue sets propagate more easily; difficulty is clue minimality, not grid size). Haiku@medium dropped cells on two puzzles and Sonnet 5.5@high dropped one cell in one replicate — the only misses recorded.

Logic puzzles (4-attribute, minimal clues) (T5B)

Design6 zebra puzzles with minimized clue sets, no tools

WhyPure deduction with irreducible clue sets. Used for reliability replicates: same set, repeated attempts per config.

FoundSeparates Haiku from everyone: every non-Haiku config solved all 6 in both replicates (one Fable@low, one Fable 5.1@medium and one Sonnet 5.5@low sample: 5/6). Haiku@high spans 3-6/6 across samples — a wide variance band where the first generation of this study saw a crisp collapse.

Strict CSV normalization (T6)

Designmessy records to exact canonical CSV under 9 interacting rules

WhyAttention-to-detail transform: date normalization, dedupe-by-latest, conditional quoting, multi-key sort, invalid-row filtering. Exact line-by-line grading.

FoundSaturated at every tier and effort (rare frontier-low one-line slips). Rules-following transforms are safe on the cheapest configs.

Knowledge recall (T7)

Design20 unambiguous technical facts, no tools or web

WhyParametric knowledge floor: chemistry symbols, ports, encodings, RFCs, Roman numerals. Hypothesis: bigger models know more.

FoundFully saturated -- 20/20 for every config and sample. Common technical knowledge has no tier gradient in 2026.

Regex writing without testing (T8)

Design8 fullmatch patterns against trap sets, graded by execution

WhyWrite-only correctness: the model cannot test its patterns; the grader executes them against must-match / must-not-match sets.

FoundSaturated on score (8/8 everywhere; one Haiku@low and one Fable 5.1@low sample 7/8) with a large efficiency tell: cost and wall-clock vary by an order of magnitude for the same result.

Bug review without tools (T9)

Design7 planted classic bugs in a rate limiter, pure code review

WhyReview with no execution: boundary conditions, off-by-ones, mutable default sharing, clamp ordering, fractional truncation. Behavioral grading of the returned fixed module.

FoundSaturated -- every config found all 7. Classic-pattern bugs are within every tier's reach; only novel semantic bugs (see e-subtle-bugs) separate.

State simulation (T10)

Design30 sequential warehouse-robot commands, exact final state

WhySustained state tracking with conflict rules (walls, collisions, capacity, no-ops). Simulator-generated ground truth.

FoundSaturated for every config on exact state (two mid-effort samples dropped robot cells). Short-horizon state tracking is solved.

TALLY-12: two programs on an invented 12-bit register machine (T13)

DesignExecute 390 + 1072 steps of a made-up VM with saturating SUB, signed-only JLT and DIV-by-zero = 4095, and report the exact output stream, registers and memory, no tools. Shipped at the `hard` preset (the tournament's recommended canonical rung).

WhySustained, strictly sequential exact arithmetic under semantics that cannot be recalled, only read. TALLY-12 has 8 twelve-bit registers, 16 memory cells and 16 opcodes; most arithmetic wraps mod 4096 but SUB saturates at 0, DIV by zero writes 4095, JLT is the only signed comparison, ROT rotates inside 12 bits and LD/ST index memory by register value mod 16. Both programs are built around a 12-bit LCG (`MUL` by a large odd constant, `ADDI` a large odd constant) whose state drives data-dependent `JLT` branches, memory scrambling, and an inner loop whose trip count is `(state AND mask) + 1` (mask 1 in Program A, mask 7 in Program B). There is no closed form and nothing to pattern-match: every OUT value depends on every earlier step.

FoundClean in both replicates for 13 of 36 configs: Opus 5.5 from medium, Opus 5 and Fable 5.1 from xhigh, Opus 4.8 at low and xhigh, Fable 5 at xhigh. Sonnet 5.5 peaks at 90 % (high) and scores 0 at max in both replicates — thinking exhausts the 128k output budget and no answer arrives; Sonnet 5 peaks at medium (81 %), hit the one-hour wall clock at max, and never exceeds 45 % above medium. Haiku 3–6 %. The task separates the Sonnet tier from the Opus/Fable tiers cleanly and the two Sonnet generations from each other; among the top tiers it is TALLY-12's cost, not its score, that differs (Opus 5.5 @ high $0.96 vs Fable 5.1 @ high $4.16 for the same 80/80).

Cold-chain depot network (T14)

Design30-day, 5-node perishable-inventory simulation from a written operating manual: lead times, FEFO lot expiry with per-delivery shelf lives, backorders, (s, S) ordering and ratio-based central rationing with cancel-not-backlog; 60 points over 51 independently graded items.

WhyThe model receives a ~14.8 KB operating manual, a day-0 state (lots with expiry days at CENTRAL and four depots, plus one shipment already in transit), a nine-line supplier delivery schedule (each delivery with its own expiry day) and a 30 x 4 demand table, and must run the network day by day through five fixed phases (receive, expire, serve, review/order, allocate/ship). It reports depot state at days 15/22/30, per-depot horizon totals, CENTRAL totals, and the network's same-day served units, total demand and fill rate.

FoundThe grid's real separator: replicated-clean for only 7 of 36 configs — Opus 5.5 at high, xhigh and max; Fable 5.1 at xhigh and max; Sonnet 5.5 at medium, xhigh and max. Opus 5 and Opus 4.8 score 100 % in one replicate and 20–50 % in the other at nearly every effort (P(clean) 0.33–0.5); Fable 5 never holds it twice (best 70 % mean at max); Sonnet 5 tops out at 75 % (medium); Haiku 10–22 %. The failure mode is always one bookkeeping slip around a stockout or expiry day — usually at the day-18/20 demand spike — that propagates through every later checkpoint. Cheapest clean: Sonnet 5.5 @ medium, $0.20 and under two minutes.

Quarry Duel (T15)

Design200 turns (`hard` preset, the default) of a fully deterministic two-player card-and-track game, played from a rulebook rendered out of the simulator's own constants; report the card played on every turn, 19 checkpoints and a final block, no tools. The prompt prints the complete game state after turn 50, so turns 51-200 are earned independently of any slip in turns 1-50.

WhySustained multi-entity state tracking where nothing can be chunked. Every turn depends on the exact hand, deck pointer, discard order, positions, coins and stockpiles left by all previous turns: draw (with a deterministic reversed-discard reshuffle at turns 53, 98, 139 and 174 on the shipped seed, the third one triggered mid-turn by a WELL draw), pick a card by a fixed policy that reads the whole hand (one player: highest card of the most-held suit with a suit-order tie-break; the other: highest card that lands on a MINE else lowest card), move on a 12-space circular track, capture (steal the opponent's lowest card), then one of six space effects (MINE, TOLL, FORGE, SWAP, QUARRY, WELL), discard, and hand-limit enforcement. That is 200 turns x 6-8 dependent rule checks (~1,400 state updates), not decomposable into independent partial sums.

FoundReplicated-clean for 13 of 36 configs: Opus 5.5 from high, Opus 5 at high and xhigh, Opus 4.8 at low and xhigh, Fable 5 at xhigh, Fable 5.1 from medium. The cascade grader makes it bimodal below those floors: the same config lands at 30 % and 100 % in consecutive runs (Opus 5 @ medium, Sonnet 5.5 @ low, Fable 5.1 @ low), so P(clean) is the number to read. Sonnet 5.5 never holds it (best 75 % at high; 31–34 % at xhigh); Sonnet 5 overran the 128k output cap at max in both replicates, scored 0. Haiku's low-effort attempts either fail to produce JSON or score under 10 %. The 200-turn play log is where every miss starts: once a stale position or hand count enters, the checkpoints and final block follow it.

§8Method corrections & honest limits

Dataset generations & corrections

This is the study's second-generation dataset at two full replicates per configuration: every active record came through the shipped, reproducible runner (Claude Code headless path with answer validation and retry), and each config's tasks — 12 originally, 15 since the hard tier — were run twice (sample tags cc1/cc2; a few cells carry a third sample from the hard tier's n=3 pilot and haiku@medium's early self-test). Fable 5.1 (2026-09-01→02), Opus 5.5 (2026-09-26) and Sonnet 5.5 (2026-09-28→10-03, all 15 tasks) were added after the fact through the same runner at n=2, and T13–T15 were run across every config over 2026-09-27→10-03 and are scored as core tasks (the floors, map totals and scatter use all 13); one Fable 5.1 max-effort replicate hit a burst of transport failures that were quarantined as invalid and refilled on a later pass. The first-generation records (a session-bound orchestration harness) are preserved in archive/workflow-runs/. Replication materially revised three of our own published claims — Opus 4.8@low's perfect sweep, Sonnet@xhigh's ledger crossing, and "max effort never wins" — all documented in F4/F5. Two grading corrections carry forward: JSON-wrapped answers are graded on content, and transport failures are quarantined as invalid rather than scored. Two cost corrections landed 2026-09-27 and re-priced every record from stored token usage: Sonnet 5 was billed at $3/$15 instead of its $2/$10 list price (all Sonnet costs fell ~25%), and cache writes under the Claude Code harness were billed at the 5-minute rate (1.25× input) when claude -p writes 1-hour caches (2× input), which raised every other family's sweep cost 6–43% (most at low effort, where cache writes dominate). Neither correction moved any floor. The runner now also records which model actually served each attempt and quarantines records answered by a fallback model after a safety-classifier refusal — a failure mode discovered during the hard-tier tournament (see candidates/TOURNAMENT.md on the hard-tier-candidates branch).

Limits

n=2 is still small — ranges are honest but confidence intervals are not computable; the plan is to accumulate replicates over time (one runner/sweep.py --sample auto per pass). All runs came from one account on subscription serving, in four measurement windows: the original six families within three days of each other in July 2026, Fable 5.1 five weeks later, Opus 5.5 four weeks after that, and Sonnet 5.5 plus the whole hard tier over 2026-09-27 → 10-03 — so serving-side drift between windows is not controlled, and cross-window comparisons carry that caveat. Two harness changes landed with the last window and apply to every record made from 2026-09-27 (all of Sonnet 5.5, all of T13–T15): claude -p runs with an empty tool list, which removes ~20k tokens of Claude Code's own system prompt from each call — worth about $0.13 (Haiku), $0.36 (Sonnet), $0.90 (Opus 5), $1.05 (Opus 5.5), $1.25 (Fable 5) and $1.63 (Fable 5.1) per 10-task sweep at list price, so Sonnet 5.5's core-set costs are ~$0.36 lighter than a like-for-like Sonnet 5 row; and a harness budget overrun — the 128k output-token maximum or the runner's one-hour wall clock — is recorded once as a valid null answer (score 0) instead of being retried and quarantined, because a model that cannot finish inside the budget is a measured outcome. Six hard-tier cells carry that outcome: Sonnet 5 @ max on Quarry Duel (both replicates) and TALLY-12 (one), Sonnet 5.5 @ max on TALLY-12 (both) and Quarry Duel (one). The CLI did not yet know Sonnet 5.5's price at measurement time; its cost rows use the published $2/$10 list price. Costs are imputed from measured tokens at standard list prices; every family's price row and the 1-hour cache-write rate were verified against the CLI's list-basis cost fields, and recomputed costs match the CLI's own total_cost_usd to the cent. Tasks are synthetic, single-shot, ≤35 minutes, without repository context; long-horizon autonomy and agentic orchestration are not measured. Response-length headroom was raised to the models' own output ceilings; harnesses with tighter caps will fail the biggest grinds. Usage-window exhaustion during long sweeps produces transport failures — the sweep driver cleans and refills them automatically.
