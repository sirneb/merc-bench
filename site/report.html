§4Findings

Editorial synthesis of the graded data (charts and map regenerate automatically; this prose is curated). The active dataset is fully reproducible: every run came through the shipped runner, and the retired first-generation dataset (a non-reproducible orchestration harness) is preserved in archive/workflow-runs/ for comparison.

F1 · Most tasks saturate — then the cheapest config strictly wins

Eight of twelve tasks (math, knowledge recall, regex, classic-bug review, state simulation, CSV, 5-attribute puzzles, and — within a one-point noise band — code trace and the constraint gauntlet) are solved by essentially every configuration. Same-score price spreads run 5–13×. For transforms, extraction, lookups, format work and classic review: buy the cheapest configuration that has ever passed the task for you.

F2 · One wall separates the field, and each config crosses it at a different price

The ledger audit (exact aggregation over 1,400 rows, no tools) is the dataset's lone hard separator. Perfect-sweep floors: Opus 5 at medium ($2.15, 8 min), Opus 4.8 at low ($2.53, 12 min), Sonnet at xhigh ($3.25, 22 min), Fable at xhigh ($7.09, 16 min); Haiku never crosses at any effort. The floors don't follow the price list or the generation order — they're empirical properties of each model, and the only way to know yours is to measure.

F3 · Opus 5 is the value frontier

Perfect from medium through max; at low it drops exactly one gauntlet point while still solving the deep puzzles 6/6 and the subtle bugs 5/5 — the two knowledge-walls that stall other tiers. Its floor is the cheapest and nearly the fastest in the dataset. If you run one configuration for everything hard, Opus 5 @ medium is the measured answer.

F4 · Effort climbs to each config's floor, then only burns — and sometimes inverts

Above a config's floor, added effort bought zero accuracy anywhere; max completed everywhere but was strictly dominated (Fable@max: $10.49 and 26 min for the same scores as $7.09/16m). Three effort inversions replicated: Haiku's ledger score falls as effort rises, Haiku's deep puzzles collapse at high (6/6 at low and medium, 3/6 at high — replicated in both study generations), and Opus 4.8@xhigh drops ledger and trace points its own low effort doesn't. Escalate one step when a config fails; never assume more effort is safe, and never buy max.

F5 · Replication humbles single-sample crowns

The first-generation dataset crowned Fable@low the only zero-defect sweep. Re-measured through the reproducible harness, Fable drops a point at low, medium and high (the same gauntlet constraint each time) and is perfect only from xhigh — while remaining the fastest tier at every effort. Single-run cells move by a point or two between honest re-measurements; treat one-sample gaps as directional and cross-task patterns as the findings.

F6 · Cheap per token ≠ capable, but it is genuinely cheap

Haiku never produced a perfect sweep and walls hard on the ledger, yet a full 10-task pass costs $0.82–0.98 — a fifth of the nearest floor — and inside the saturated zone it matches every tier. Route mechanical work there at low effort (its effort dial only ever hurt it), and never route precision aggregation or deep deduction there.

F7 · The bill is verbosity: effort multiplies tokens for flat scores

Within every family, sweep cost rises 1.2–2.8× from low to max for identical or worse results (Sonnet $1.70→$4.38; Opus 5 $1.93→$3.65; Fable $3.69→$10.49). Time behaves the same (Opus 5: 7→17 min; Sonnet: 10→33 min). Grind-prone precision work belongs at the frontier tier's floor — or give the model tools: in the archived tool-enabled rounds, execution access saturated these tasks for every tier.

F8 · The generation gap lives in novel semantics, not classic patterns

Opus 4.8 matches Opus 5's sweeps at startlingly low cost (its low-effort sweep is the #2 value in the dataset) — but on the subtle semantic bugs it stalls at 4/5 until xhigh, in both study generations, while Opus 5 and Fable find 5/5 at every effort. Classic-pattern review (T9) saturates for everyone. Route novelty — unfamiliar semantics, first-of-kind review — to the newest generation; route familiar patterns to whatever is cheapest.

§5The tasks

One card per task: design, rationale, and verdict. Full prompts, schemas and self-contained graders live in tasks/.

Subtle semantic bugs (E)

Design5 planted conceptual bugs in a search index

WhyNovel semantic defects (document-frequency semantics, operator precedence, case asymmetry, phrase adjacency, normalization denominator) that resist pattern-matching review.

FoundThe generation wall: Opus 4.8 stalls at 4/5 below xhigh in both study generations; Opus 5 and Fable find 5/5 at every effort. Sonnet crosses at high; Haiku is noisy (3-5/5).

Mental math (T1)

Design16 exact computations, no tools

WhyTests exact arithmetic under load: 4-digit products, signed sums, modular exponentiation, compound percentages. Hypothesis: tier gaps and effort gains on raw computation.

FoundNear-saturated: only Haiku drops an item (n=1 cells). Effort spends multiples of tokens for identical scores. 2026 models simply do arithmetic.

Code trace (T2)

Designpredict exact stdout of 8 Python snippets

WhyPython semantics traps (mutable defaults, closure late binding, dict key collapse, try/finally returns, generator exhaustion). Ground truth produced by executing the snippets.

FoundA one-point noise band: scattered single misses across Haiku (all efforts), Sonnet low/medium and Opus 4.8@xhigh; perfect elsewhere. No config missed two.

Ledger audit (T3)

Design6 exact aggregates over 1,400 transaction rows, no tools

WhyPrecision aggregation at scale: balances, filtered counts, running-threshold detection, maxima, group sums, busiest-day counts. The strongest separator in the study.

FoundThe wall battery — the dataset's lone hard separator. Perfect-sweep floors: Opus 5@medium, Opus 4.8@low, Sonnet@xhigh, Fable@xhigh; Haiku never crosses and its score falls as effort rises. The biggest token grinds live here.

Constraint gauntlet (T4)

Designone essay satisfying 10 mechanical constraints

WhyInstruction-following under load: exact word counts, an e-free paragraph, exact substring counts, ordered first words, length caps -- all machine-checkable.

FoundNear-saturated (a published correction: the original "Sonnet 3/10 to 10/10 effort ladder" was a grading artifact from scoring JSON-wrapped text). Every tier above Haiku scores 9-10/10; Fable persistently dropped the same single constraint below xhigh; Haiku drops 1-2.

Logic puzzles (5-attribute) (T5A)

Design3 zebra puzzles, 25 cells each, solver-verified unique, no tools

WhyDeduction with a bigger grid. Hypothesis: scale deepens the reasoning wall.

FoundHypothesis rejected -- saturated for every config above Haiku (richer clue sets propagate more easily; difficulty is clue minimality, not grid size). Haiku@medium dropped cells on two puzzles, the only miss recorded.

Logic puzzles (4-attribute, minimal clues) (T5B)

Design6 zebra puzzles with minimized clue sets, no tools

WhyPure deduction with irreducible clue sets. Used for reliability replicates: same set, repeated attempts per config.

FoundSeparates by effort direction, not tier: Haiku solves all 6 at low and medium but collapses at high (replicated in both study generations); every other config is perfect except one Fable@low sample (5/6).

Strict CSV normalization (T6)

Designmessy records to exact canonical CSV under 9 interacting rules

WhyAttention-to-detail transform: date normalization, dedupe-by-latest, conditional quoting, multi-key sort, invalid-row filtering. Exact line-by-line grading.

FoundSaturated at every tier and effort (rare frontier-low one-line slips). Rules-following transforms are safe on the cheapest configs.

Knowledge recall (T7)

Design20 unambiguous technical facts, no tools or web

WhyParametric knowledge floor: chemistry symbols, ports, encodings, RFCs, Roman numerals. Hypothesis: bigger models know more.

FoundFully saturated -- 20/20 for every config and sample. Common technical knowledge has no tier gradient in 2026.

Regex writing without testing (T8)

Design8 fullmatch patterns against trap sets, graded by execution

WhyWrite-only correctness: the model cannot test its patterns; the grader executes them against must-match / must-not-match sets.

FoundSaturated on score (8/8 everywhere, one Haiku@low 7/8) with a large efficiency tell: cost and wall-clock vary by an order of magnitude for the same result.

Bug review without tools (T9)

Design7 planted classic bugs in a rate limiter, pure code review

WhyReview with no execution: boundary conditions, off-by-ones, mutable default sharing, clamp ordering, fractional truncation. Behavioral grading of the returned fixed module.

FoundSaturated -- every config found all 7. Classic-pattern bugs are within every tier's reach; only novel semantic bugs (see e-subtle-bugs) separate.

State simulation (T10)

Design30 sequential warehouse-robot commands, exact final state

WhySustained state tracking with conflict rules (walls, collisions, capacity, no-ops). Simulator-generated ground truth.

FoundSaturated for every config on exact state (two mid-effort samples dropped robot cells). Short-horizon state tracking is solved.

§8Method corrections & honest limits

Dataset generations & corrections

This is the study's second-generation dataset: every active record was produced through the shipped, reproducible runner (Claude Code headless path with answer validation and retry), re-measuring the full grid that the first-generation study ran through a session-bound orchestration harness. The first-generation records are preserved in archive/workflow-runs/; headline agreements (the Opus 5@medium floor, the Sonnet xhigh ledger crossing, Haiku's deep-puzzle collapse at high effort, Opus 4.8's subtle-bugs stall) and disagreements (Fable@low's zero-defect sweep did not replicate; Opus 4.8@low swept perfect here) are discussed in the findings. Two grading corrections from the first generation carry forward: JSON-wrapped answers are graded on content (the original T4 "effort ladder" was an artifact of scoring the wrapper), and transport failures are quarantined as invalid rather than scored.

Limits

Every active cell is n=1 (haiku@medium n=2 on several tasks) from a single account over two days on subscription serving; costs are imputed from measured tokens at standard list prices. Tasks are synthetic, single-shot, ≤35 minutes, without repository context; long-horizon autonomy and agentic orchestration are not measured. Response-length headroom was raised to the models' own output ceilings; harnesses with tighter caps will fail the biggest grinds. Rerun before trusting fine margins — one command per config reruns any cell.
