Most tasks saturate

cheapest config clean in every replicate of the 10 core tasks: Sonnet 5 max ($3.55/33m) · Sonnet 5.5 xhigh ($1.29/9m) · Opus 5 medium ($2.76/9m) · Opus 5.5 xhigh ($3.08/10m) · Fable 5 max ($11.53/27m) · Fable 5.1 xhigh ($8.03/16m). §2How this was measured

ControlHow
Reproducible harnessEvery active record came through the shipped runner (runner/run.py, Claude Code headless path) with answer validation and up to 3 corrective retries; transport failures are quarantined as invalid, never scored. One command reruns any cell; runner/sweep.py reruns the whole grid.
Ground truth firstReference implementations cross-checked against independently-written oracles and fuzzing (which caught two harness bugs before any model ran); solver-verified unique puzzles; executed code snippets; generator-computed ledger and simulation answers. All ground truth ships in tasks/*/.
One variable at a timeIdentical prompts per task across every configuration; only the model or its effort setting changes. Prompts ship verbatim in tasks/*/prompt.txt.
Grading recomputedNothing is trusted from stored records except raw answers. runner/aggregate.py regrades every answer through the shipped graders on every build; make verify is the audit path.
Cost accountingPer-run dollars (input + cache writes + cache reads + output, including retries) from the CLI's per-run usage report, priced at standard list rates, with cache writes at the harness's TTL rate (1-hour, 2× input, under Claude Code) and every price row verified against the CLI's list-basis cost fields. Costs are recomputed from stored token usage on every build. Subscription serving — costs are imputed, not billed.
No toolsTasks forbid tool use (tool access lets every tier self-verify to perfection, masking differences — measured in the archived tool-enabled rounds).

§4Findings

Editorial synthesis of the graded data (charts and map regenerate automatically; this prose is curated). Every configuration now carries two full replicates through the reproducible runner; the retired first-generation dataset (a non-reproducible orchestration harness) is preserved in archive/workflow-runs/.

F1 · Most tasks saturate — then the cheapest config strictly wins

Knowledge recall, classic-bug review, state simulation, 5-attribute puzzles and regex writing were solved by essentially every configuration in every replicate; math, code trace, CSV and the constraint gauntlet sit within a one-or-two-point noise band for everything above Haiku. Same-score price spreads run 5–12×. For transforms, extraction, lookups, format work and classic review: buy the cheapest configuration that has passed the task for you.

F2 · Reliability floors, measured with replicates

Defining a config as clean only if it dropped zero points in every replicate of the 10 core tasks: Opus 5 is clean from medium up ($2.76, 9 min — the cheapest and nearly fastest clean config in the grid); Opus 5.5 from xhigh up ($3.08, 10 min); Fable 5.1 from xhigh up ($8.03, 16 min); Sonnet is clean only at max ($3.55, 33 min); Fable 5 only at max ($11.53, 27 min); Opus 4.8 and Haiku were never clean at any effort. Four of the ten replicated-clean configs in the entire grid belong to Opus 5; the two point releases added later (Fable 5.1, Opus 5.5) contribute two each.

F3 · Opus 5 is the value frontier, and replication strengthened it

Clean twice at medium, high, xhigh and max; at low it dropped exactly one gauntlet point — the same point — in both samples, while still solving the deep puzzles 6/6 and the subtle bugs 5/5 twice. Every other family got less reliable under replication; Opus 5 got confirmed. If you run one configuration for everything hard, Opus 5 @ medium is the measured answer. Opus 5.5, measured two months later at a lower list price ($4/$20), lands 32 cents behind it: clean at xhigh ($3.08, 10 min) and max, with one gauntlet point dropped in one replicate at medium and high and a ledger point (4–5/6) at low. Below its floor it is the fastest thing in the grid — a full low-effort sweep is 3 minutes and $1.78, a quarter less than Opus 5@low — so the choice between the two Opus generations is "clean at medium for $2.76" versus "nearly clean at low for $1.78".

F4 · Effort buys the last point — at the wrong price

Replication revised our own earlier claim that max never wins: max is precisely where Sonnet and Fable first went zero-defect in both samples (Sonnet's xhigh and Fable's xhigh each still dropped a point in one replicate). But the last point is overpriced: Fable 5's ladder to its clean config runs $4.98→$11.53, Fable 5.1's $4.99→$8.03 (its slip clears one step earlier, at xhigh), Sonnet's $1.42→$3.55, and Opus 5.5's $1.78→$3.08 for xhigh and $7.71 for max — the steepest low→max multiplier in the grid (4.3×) for scores identical to xhigh. Opus 5 delivers replicated zero-defect at $2.76/9 min. Below the last point, effort mostly bought nothing; on Haiku it correlated with worse ledger scores (best sample 5/6 at low vs 2/6-band at high). Escalate one step when a config fails; buy the tier, not the grind, when you need clean.

F5 · Single-sample crowns die under replication

Three cc1 headliners regressed at n=2: Opus 4.8@low's perfect sweep (second ledger sample: 3/6 — its range is now 3–6 at every effort), Sonnet@xhigh's ledger crossing (5–6), and Haiku's crisp "effort inversions" (now wide variance bands: T3 2–5, T6 7–13, deep puzzles 3–6 at high). What replicated instead: Opus 5's cleanliness, the E generation wall, and Fable's stubborn single gauntlet point at every effort below max — a slip Fable 5.1 then reproduced at every effort below xhigh. Treat any n=1 surprise as a hypothesis; this repository exists so you can rerun it.

F6 · Cheap per token ≠ capable, but it is genuinely cheap

Haiku was never clean and its variance bands are the widest in the grid, yet a full 10-task sweep costs $0.93–1.21 — a fraction of the nearest floor — and inside the saturated zone it matched every tier in both samples. Route mechanical work there at low effort; never route precision aggregation or deep deduction there.

F7 · The bill is verbosity: effort multiplies cost for the same scores

Within every family, sweep cost rises 1.7–4.3× from low to max (Sonnet $1.42→$3.55; Opus 5 $2.51→$4.30; Opus 5.5 $1.78→$7.71; Fable 5 $4.98→$11.53; Fable 5.1 $4.99→$12.00) and time rises with it (Opus 5: 7→18 min; Opus 5.5: 3→33 min; Sonnet: 11→33 min) — almost always for identical scores. Opus 5.5 is the sharpest example: max costs 2.5× its already-clean xhigh and takes three times as long for the same 100/100. Grind-prone precision work belongs at Opus 5's floor — or give the model tools: in the archived tool-enabled rounds, execution access saturated these tasks for every tier.

F8 · The generation gap lives in novel semantics — and in reliability

Opus 4.8 stalls at 4/5 on the subtle semantic bugs at low and high and fixes the fifth only at xhigh — in all four samples across both study generations — while Opus 5 and Fable found 5/5 at every effort, twice. And at the same list price as Opus 5, Opus 4.8 never produced a clean sweep. The same-price generation upgrade is free reliability; take it and re-find your floor a step or two lower. The point releases replicated the pattern in miniature: Fable 5.1 kept 5/5 on the subtle bugs at every effort and moved Fable's clean point down one step (xhigh instead of max); Opus 5.5 also found 5/5 at every effort, but its cheaper list price is paid back in effort — it needs xhigh to be clean where Opus 5 needs medium. Re-find the floor after every release, in both directions.

§5The tasks

One card per task: design, rationale, and verdict. Full prompts, schemas and self-contained graders live in tasks/.

Subtle semantic bugs (E)

Design5 planted conceptual bugs in a search index

WhyNovel semantic defects (document-frequency semantics, operator precedence, case asymmetry, phrase adjacency, normalization denominator) that resist pattern-matching review.

FoundThe generation wall: Opus 4.8 stalls at 4/5 below xhigh in both study generations; Opus 5, Opus 5.5, Fable 5 and Fable 5.1 find 5/5 at every effort. Sonnet crosses at high; Haiku is noisy (3-5/5).

Mental math (T1)

Design16 exact computations, no tools

WhyTests exact arithmetic under load: 4-digit products, signed sums, modular exponentiation, compound percentages. Hypothesis: tier gaps and effort gains on raw computation.

FoundNear-saturated: only Haiku drops an item (n=1 cells). Effort spends multiples of tokens for identical scores. 2026 models simply do arithmetic.

Code trace (T2)

Designpredict exact stdout of 8 Python snippets

WhyPython semantics traps (mutable defaults, closure late binding, dict key collapse, try/finally returns, generator exhaustion). Ground truth produced by executing the snippets.

FoundA one-point noise band: scattered single misses across Haiku (all efforts), Sonnet low/medium and Opus 4.8@xhigh; perfect elsewhere. No config missed two.

Ledger audit (T3)

Design6 exact aggregates over 1,400 transaction rows, no tools

WhyPrecision aggregation at scale: balances, filtered counts, running-threshold detection, maxima, group sums, busiest-day counts. The strongest separator in the study.

FoundThe wall battery — the dataset's lone hard separator, and the noisiest cell in the grid: Opus 4.8 spans 3-6/6 across replicates at every effort, Haiku 2-5/6. Only Opus 5 (medium up), Opus 5.5 (medium up), Fable 5.1 (every effort), Sonnet@max and Fable 5@max held 6/6 in both replicates; Opus 5.5@low dropped one (4-5/6). The biggest token grinds live here.

Constraint gauntlet (T4)

Designone essay satisfying 10 mechanical constraints

WhyInstruction-following under load: exact word counts, an e-free paragraph, exact substring counts, ordered first words, length caps -- all machine-checkable.

FoundNear-saturated (a published correction: the original "Sonnet 3/10 to 10/10 effort ladder" was a grading artifact from scoring JSON-wrapped text). Every tier above Haiku scores 9-10/10; Fable 5 persistently dropped the same single constraint at every effort below max, in both replicates; Fable 5.1 does the same below xhigh; Opus 5.5 dropped it in one replicate at medium and high (clean at low, xhigh and max); Haiku drops 1-2.

Logic puzzles (5-attribute) (T5A)

Design3 zebra puzzles, 25 cells each, solver-verified unique, no tools

WhyDeduction with a bigger grid. Hypothesis: scale deepens the reasoning wall.

FoundHypothesis rejected -- saturated for every config above Haiku (richer clue sets propagate more easily; difficulty is clue minimality, not grid size). Haiku@medium dropped cells on two puzzles, the only miss recorded.

Logic puzzles (4-attribute, minimal clues) (T5B)

Design6 zebra puzzles with minimized clue sets, no tools

WhyPure deduction with irreducible clue sets. Used for reliability replicates: same set, repeated attempts per config.

FoundSeparates Haiku from everyone: every non-Haiku config solved all 6 in both replicates (one Fable@low sample and one Fable 5.1@medium sample: 5/6). Haiku@high spans 3-6/6 across samples — a wide variance band where the first generation of this study saw a crisp collapse.

Strict CSV normalization (T6)

Designmessy records to exact canonical CSV under 9 interacting rules

WhyAttention-to-detail transform: date normalization, dedupe-by-latest, conditional quoting, multi-key sort, invalid-row filtering. Exact line-by-line grading.

FoundSaturated at every tier and effort (rare frontier-low one-line slips). Rules-following transforms are safe on the cheapest configs.

Knowledge recall (T7)

Design20 unambiguous technical facts, no tools or web

WhyParametric knowledge floor: chemistry symbols, ports, encodings, RFCs, Roman numerals. Hypothesis: bigger models know more.

FoundFully saturated -- 20/20 for every config and sample. Common technical knowledge has no tier gradient in 2026.

Regex writing without testing (T8)

Design8 fullmatch patterns against trap sets, graded by execution

TALLY-12: two programs on an invented 12-bit register machine (T13)

DesignExecute 390 + 1072 steps of a made-up VM with saturating SUB, signed-only JLT and DIV-by-zero = 4095, and report the exact output stream, registers and memory, no tools. Shipped at the `hard` preset (the tournament's recommended canonical rung).

WhySustained, strictly sequential exact arithmetic under semantics that cannot be recalled, only read. TALLY-12 has 8 twelve-bit registers, 16 memory cells and 16 opcodes; most arithmetic wraps mod 4096 but SUB saturates at 0, DIV by zero writes 4095, JLT is the only signed comparison, ROT rotates inside 12 bits and LD/ST index memory by register value mod 16. Both programs are built around a 12-bit LCG (`MUL` by a large odd constant, `ADDI` a large odd constant) whose state drives data-dependent `JLT` branches, memory scrambling, and an inner loop whose trip count is `(state AND mask) + 1` (mask 1 in Program A, mask 7 in Program B). There is no closed form and nothing to pattern-match: every OUT value depends on every earlier step.

Quarry Duel (T15)

Design200 turns (`hard` preset, the default) of a fully deterministic two-player card-and-track game, played from a rulebook rendered out of the simulator's own constants; report the card played on every turn, 19 checkpoints and a final block, no tools. §8Method corrections & honest limits

Dataset generations & corrections

This is the study's second-generation dataset at two full replicates per configuration: every active record came through the shipped, reproducible runner (Claude Code headless path with answer validation and retry), and each config's 12 tasks were run twice (sample tags cc1/cc2; a few haiku@medium cells carry a third early sample). Fable 5.1 (2026-09-01→02) and Opus 5.5 (2026-09-26) were added after the fact through the same runner at n=2; one Fable 5.1 max-effort replicate hit a burst of transport failures that were quarantined as invalid and refilled on a later pass. The first-generation records (a session-bound orchestration harness) are preserved in archive/workflow-runs/. Replication materially revised three of our own published claims — Opus 4.8@low's perfect sweep, Sonnet@xhigh's ledger crossing, and "max effort never wins" — all documented in F4/F5. Two grading corrections carry forward: JSON-wrapped answers are graded on content, and transport failures are quarantined as invalid rather than scored. Two cost corrections landed 2026-09-27 and re-priced every record from stored token usage: Sonnet 5 was billed at $3/$15 instead of its $2/$10 list price (all Sonnet costs fell ~25%), and cache writes under the Claude Code harness were billed at the 5-minute rate (1.25× input) when claude -p writes 1-hour caches (2× input), which raised every other family's sweep cost 6–43% (most at low effort, where cache writes dominate). Neither correction moved any floor. The runner now also records which model actually served each attempt and quarantines records answered by a fallback model after a safety-classifier refusal — a failure mode discovered during the hard-tier tournament (see candidates/TOURNAMENT.md on the hard-tier-candidates branch).

Limits

n=2 is still small — ranges are honest but confidence intervals are not computable; the plan is to accumulate replicates over time (one runner/sweep.py --sample auto per pass). All runs came from one account on subscription serving, in three measurement windows: the original six families within three days of each other in July 2026, Fable 5.1 five weeks later, Opus 5.5 four weeks after that — so serving-side drift between windows is not controlled, and cross-window comparisons (Opus 5 vs Opus 5.5, Fable 5 vs Fable 5.1) carry that caveat. Costs are imputed from measured tokens at standard list prices; every family's price row and the 1-hour cache-write rate were verified against the CLI's list-basis cost fields, and recomputed costs match the CLI's own total_cost_usd to the cent. Tasks are synthetic, single-shot, ≤35 minutes, without repository context; long-horizon autonomy and agentic orchestration are not measured. Response-length headroom was raised to the models' own output ceilings; harnesses with tighter caps will fail the biggest grinds. Usage-window exhaustion during long sweeps produces transport failures — the sweep driver cleans and refills them automatically.
